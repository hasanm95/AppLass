**Facebook doesn't let you turn Reels off.** You can tell it to show you fewer, and you can set a
reminder for your total time on Facebook, but neither stops the scroll. To keep groups, Marketplace
and your feed and cap only Reels, you need a limit that works on the Reels screen itself.

## Why Facebook Reels are hard to ignore

Reels now show up in several places: a Reels section of their own, and suggested Reels mixed into
your main feed. Tap one and you are in a full-screen, endless feed that works just like Instagram
Reels. You came for a group post or a Marketplace message and ended up twenty videos in.

It is a growing share of the app, too. Sensor Tower data reported by WARC puts Reels at about **29%
of the time US users spent on Facebook in 2025**, up from the year before
([WARC](https://www.warc.com/en/article/short-form-video-is-driving-record-engagement-across-metas-platforms-41fb304cb3614b56b706f64dde1d6a52)).

## How to turn off Reels on Facebook: what the app lets you change

There is no permanent off switch, but these settings cut down how often Reels reach you:

1. **Hide the Reels section.** When a Reels row appears in your feed, tap its **three dots** and choose
   **Hide**. Reels show up less often afterwards, though not never.
2. **Turn off video autoplay.** Go to **Menu → Settings & privacy → Settings → Media** and set
   **Autoplay** to **Never autoplay videos**. Reels in the feed no longer start on their own.
3. **"Show less" and hide by Page.** Tap the three dots on a Reel to see fewer like it, or hide all
   Reels from a particular Page. This changes *which* Reels you get, not how many.
4. **Check "Your time on Facebook".** Under **Settings & privacy**, it shows your time in the app and can
   remind you. It covers all of Facebook, and the reminder can be dismissed with a tap.

Menu names move around between app versions, so search Facebook's settings if you can't find one. None
of these stops the scroll once you open a Reel — for that you need a limit on the Reels feed itself.

## What Android's app timer does

Android's **Digital Wellbeing** (Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls) lets you set an
**app timer** on Facebook. When it runs out, Facebook is greyed out until midnight.

Messenger is a separate app on Android, so your chats keep working. Everything else in Facebook
stops, though: groups, Marketplace, events and notifications from Pages you rely on. And the timer
is easy to raise again in Settings when you want "just a bit more".

## How to cap only the Reels feed on Android

A feed-level limit closes Facebook Reels and leaves the rest of the app alone. With
[Mindful Guard](/apps/mindful-guard):

1. Install Mindful Guard and allow the **Accessibility Service** when asked. It uses it to notice
   when the Reels feed opens.
2. Open **Reel Guard** and turn on **Facebook Reels**.
3. Set a **daily limit**, a **per-session limit**, or both.
4. Optionally add a cooldown between sessions.

When you hit the limit, Reels close and you are back in Facebook. Groups, Marketplace, events and
your feed keep working.

## Choosing your numbers

| Goal | Daily limit | Per-session limit |
|---|---|---|
| Cut back gently | 20 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Mostly quit | 5 minutes | 2 minutes |
| Quit completely | 0 minutes | — |

A daily limit on its own is easy to spend in one long sitting. The per-session limit is what breaks
that sitting early. More on this in [the session method for limiting Reels](/blog/limit-reels-per-day-android).

## If you also use Instagram

Facebook Reels and Instagram Reels pull from the same pool of videos, so limiting one often pushes
you to the other. Reel Guard sets separate limits for each, plus YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Snapchat
Spotlight. See [how to block Instagram Reels on Android](/blog/how-to-block-instagram-reels-android).

## The bottom line

Facebook's own tools change what Reels you see and remind you about time, but they don't stop the
feed. An app timer stops all of Facebook. A feed-level limit is the option that caps Reels and keeps
the parts of Facebook you came for.
