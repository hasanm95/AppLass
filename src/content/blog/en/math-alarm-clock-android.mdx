**A math alarm keeps ringing until you solve a problem on the screen.** You can't do that on autopilot, so
you can't switch it off half-asleep the way you can with a normal off button. By the time you've solved it,
you're past the worst of the post-waking fog and much less likely to fall back asleep.

## Why the off button is the problem

Most people who sleep through alarms don't miss the sound. They switch it off without fully waking up, and
don't remember doing it. A one-tap off or snooze button is easy to hit while your brain is still mostly
asleep.

## Why solving math works: sleep inertia

The fog right after waking has a name: **sleep inertia**. A University of Colorado study published in
*JAMA* found the most severe impairment in the **first three minutes** after waking, with most of it gone
within about **10 minutes** — though some effects lasted up to two hours
([University of Colorado](https://www.colorado.edu/today/2006/01/10/grogginess-disorientation-awakening-more-debilitating-sleep-deprivation-according-new-cu)).
A review for the European Union Aviation Safety Agency found sleep inertia lasting from **0 to 35 minutes**
depending on the type of sleep you wake from
([EASA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/139387/en)).

A math problem keeps you engaged right through those first minutes. The alarm doesn't stop until your
brain is awake enough to solve it — and by then, staying up is much easier. More tips in
[how to stop sleeping through your alarm](/blog/how-to-stop-sleeping-through-your-alarm-android).

## Android's Clock app doesn't have one

Google's and Samsung's built-in Clock apps let you set the time, sound, vibration and snooze, but there's
no math task. For that you need a separate alarm app. Several exist, such as Alarmy and dedicated math
alarm apps; Arisely includes Math as one of its ten wake-up tasks.

## Set up a math alarm with Arisely

[Arisely](/blog/arisely-alarm-clock-for-heavy-sleepers) is an Android alarm clock for heavy sleepers. It
keeps ringing at full volume until you finish the wake-up task you chose, which Arisely calls a **Ritual**.

1. Install Arisely and create an alarm. Use **Repeat** for workdays.
2. Tap **Ritual**, choose **Math** and pick a difficulty: **Easy**, **Medium** or **Hard**.
3. Choose a sound — a ringtone, or a song from **My Music**.
4. Keep the **volume** high. Arisely rings on Android's alarm channel, so it is loud even when media volume
   is down or Do Not Disturb is on.
5. Set snooze the way you want: a length and a limit, or off entirely.

## Choosing the difficulty

| You… | Try |
|---|---|
| Wake up fairly easily but hit snooze | Easy |
| Switch alarms off without remembering | Medium |
| Can solve Medium half-asleep | Hard |
| Still fall back asleep after solving | Add a physical task on hard days |

The right level makes you think for a minute or two. If you're solving it on autopilot, go up a level; if
you're so frustrated that you start dreading or skipping the alarm, go down.

## When math isn't enough

Some heavy sleepers solve the problem and go straight back to bed. If that's you, combine the brain task
with a body task: get up and walk, scan a code in another room, or do push-ups the camera counts. Arisely
has these as Rituals too — see [alarm clocks with missions](/blog/alarm-clock-with-missions-to-turn-off)
and [the push-up alarm](/blog/alarm-app-that-makes-you-do-pushups-android).

And check the alarm actually fires: [why Android alarms don't go off](/blog/why-android-alarms-dont-go-off-exact-alarms).

## The bottom line

A math alarm takes the off button out of reach of your sleeping brain. Pick a difficulty that makes you
think for a minute, keep the volume up, and add a physical task on the mornings when math alone isn't
enough.
