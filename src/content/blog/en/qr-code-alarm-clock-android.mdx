**A QR code alarm only stops when you scan a code you put somewhere else.** You can't silence it from
bed, so you have to get up, walk to the code and aim the camera at it. By the time it stops ringing,
you are on your feet and awake enough not to crawl back under the covers.

## Why the off button is the problem

Most people who sleep through alarms don't actually sleep through the sound. They switch it off
without fully waking up, and don't remember doing it. A one-tap off button or a snooze button is
easy to hit half-asleep.

Putting the phone across the room helps for a while, until you learn to walk over, switch it off and
go back to bed. A barcode alarm takes this one step further: the phone can stay by your bed, but the
**code** is somewhere else, and only the code turns the alarm off.

## How a barcode alarm works

1. When you set the alarm, you choose the code it will accept: a QR code or a barcode on something
   you own.
2. In the morning the alarm rings and keeps ringing.
3. To stop it, you walk to the code and scan it with the phone's camera.
4. Any other code, or no code, and it keeps going.

Two things wake you up here. **Moving** gets your body upright and your blood flowing. **Aiming the
camera** needs just enough focus that you can't do it on autopilot.

## Where to put the code

Pick a spot that is far enough to need a few steps, and that leads into your morning:

| Spot | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Bathroom sink | You're already where you'd brush your teeth |
| Coffee maker or kettle | Scan, then start the coffee |
| Kitchen counter | Furthest from the bed in most homes |
| Front door | Good for early runs or commutes |

Use an item that **doesn't move**: a shampoo bottle, a coffee packet, a sticker on a cupboard door. If
the code isn't there in the morning, the alarm won't stop, so keep it somewhere it can't wander off.
And keep the spot reasonably lit, or switch on a light on your way: that helps you wake up too.

## Make it stick

- **Don't keep a spare code by the bed.** A photo of the code on another screen defeats the point.
- **Combine Rituals on hard days.** If one scan isn't enough, other Rituals like steps, math or
  push-ups the camera counts can be used on other alarms. See
  [alarm clocks with missions](/blog/alarm-clock-with-missions-to-turn-off).
- **Fix the basics too.** A barcode alarm won't help if the alarm doesn't fire. Make sure Android
  isn't blocking it: [why Android alarms don't go off](/blog/why-android-alarms-dont-go-off-exact-alarms).

## The bottom line

If you switch off alarms in your sleep, take the off switch out of reach. A QR or barcode alarm puts
it across the room, behind a few steps and a moment of focus, which is usually enough to keep you up.
