**A black screen app makes your screen look off while the phone stays unlocked.** It draws a pure black
layer over everything, so the video, lecture or app underneath keeps running, and it blocks touches until
you unlock on purpose. It's the workaround for something Android can't do on its own: turn the screen off
without locking.

## Why you can't just press the power button

On Android, turning the screen off and locking the phone are the same action. Once the phone locks, the app
on screen moves to the background, and many apps pause there. YouTube without Premium is the best-known
example; browsers, course apps and many video players do the same.

A black screen app keeps the phone unlocked and the app in front. To Android nothing has changed — you just
can't see it.

## What people use black screen apps for

- **Listening to videos** — lectures, podcasts with video, long talks — on a walk or a commute.
- **Falling asleep** to something playing without a glowing screen.
- **Keeping a phone in a pocket** without touches pausing or skipping anything.
- **Hiding the screen** while a recording, a download or a navigation app keeps running.
- **Saving battery** on AMOLED phones while audio plays.

## What to look for

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **No root needed** | Works on any phone, without modifying it |
| **Touch blocking** | Stops a pocket or sleeve from tapping the app underneath |
| **Easy, deliberate unlock** | You can get back in quickly, but not by accident |
| **Quick start** | A floating button or Quick Settings tile, so it's one tap |
| **Pure black** | On AMOLED, true black pixels are switched off |
| **Few permissions** | An overlay only needs "display over other apps" |
| **Extras you'll use** | A clock on black, a sleep timer, pocket mode |

**Your main options:**

- **Overlay apps** (no root), such as ScreenVeil or Blackr: draw black over any app. On LCD screens the
  backlight stays on.
- **Backlight apps** (root only), such as Screen Backlight Off, which switch the backlight itself off
  ([XDA](https://forum.xda-developers.com/t/app-2-3-root-screen-backlight-off-xda-exclusive-free-version.3037380/post-59031010)).
- **An app's own background mode**, such as YouTube Premium or VLC's audio playback — only for that app.
  For YouTube, see [YouTube background play in 2026](/blog/youtube-background-play-2026-workaround).

## How to use a black screen app (ScreenVeil)

[ScreenVeil](/apps/screenveil) is a black screen overlay for Android:

1. Install ScreenVeil and tap **Start Service**.
2. Allow **"display over other apps"** when Android asks. It is the only permission you grant.
3. Open the app you want to keep running and start it.
4. Tap ScreenVeil's floating button or its Quick Settings tile. The screen turns black.
5. To get back, touch the screen and tap unlock. With biometric unlock on, only your fingerprint lifts the
   black screen.

ScreenVeil also has a **dim mode** (a low glow instead of full black), a **sleep timer**, **pocket mode**
that turns the screen black when the phone goes into your pocket, and an optional **clock and date** on the
black screen.

## Battery: what to expect

| Screen type | Black screen app | Saving |
|---|---|---|
| AMOLED / OLED | Black pixels are switched off | Large, especially at high brightness |
| LCD | Backlight stays on behind the black | Small |

The brighter your screen was and the longer you keep it black, the more you save on AMOLED. For the
details, see [does dark mode save battery on AMOLED?](/blog/does-dark-mode-save-battery-amoled)

## The bottom line

Android can't turn the screen off without locking, and locking stops many apps. A black screen app fills
that gap: the screen looks off, the app keeps running and touches are blocked. Pick one that needs no root,
blocks touches and asks only for "display over other apps".
