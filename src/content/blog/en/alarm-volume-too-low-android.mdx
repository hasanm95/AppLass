**Android keeps a separate volume for alarms.** Your music and ringtone can be loud while the alarm volume
sits near the bottom. Raise the alarm slider in the volume panel or Sound settings, check the alarm's own
volume in the Clock app, turn off the gradual increase if the start is too quiet, and pick a louder sound.

## Step 1: Raise the alarm volume

Android has separate sliders for media, calls, ringtone and alarms
([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/why-is-my-alarm-so-quiet)):

- **Quickest:** press a volume button, then tap the **settings** or **three-dot** icon on the volume panel
  and raise the **Alarm** slider.
- **Pixel and most phones:** Settings → **Sound & vibration** → **Alarm volume**.
- **Samsung:** Settings → **Sounds and vibration** → **Volume** → **Alarm**.

Google's Clock app even warns you if an alarm went off with the volume below 30%
([Google Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/2840926)).

## Step 2: Check the alarm's own volume

Some Clock apps have a volume per alarm, on top of the system slider:

- **Samsung Clock:** open the alarm → **Alarm sound** → move the volume slider
  ([Samsung Community](https://eu.community.samsung.com:443/t5/other-galaxy-s-series/alarm-volume/td-p/3251252)).
- **Google Clock:** More → **Settings** → **Alarms** → alarm volume.

## Step 3: Check "Gradually increase volume"

If your alarm starts almost silent and only gets loud later, that's the gradual-increase setting. In
Google's Clock app: More → **Settings** → **Alarms** → **Gradually increase volume**
([Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/2841106)). Turn it off, or shorten the ramp, if
you sleep through the quiet start.

## Step 4: Pick a louder sound

Soft or slow tones are quieter at the same volume. Choose a tone with a sharp, high start, or a song you
know well. Try it at full volume during the day before relying on it.

## Step 5: Rule out Battery Saver and Do Not Disturb

- **Battery Saver** can stop an alarm from going off properly. Google recommends turning it off or adding
  Clock as an essential app ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/2840926)).
- **Do Not Disturb** lets alarms through by default. Check that the **Alarms** exception is still on.

If the alarm doesn't ring at all rather than ringing quietly, see
[why Android alarms don't go off](/blog/why-android-alarms-dont-go-off-exact-alarms).

## Quick checklist

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Alarm quiet, music loud | Raise the separate **Alarm** slider |
| One alarm quieter than others | Check that alarm's own volume in the Clock app |
| Starts silent, gets loud later | Turn off **Gradually increase volume** |
| Still too soft at full volume | Choose a sharper tone or a song you know |
| Alarm sometimes doesn't ring | Turn off Battery Saver for Clock; check Do Not Disturb |

## The bottom line

A quiet Android alarm is almost always the separate alarm volume, the alarm's own slider, or the
gradual-increase setting. Fix those first. If you still sleep through a loud alarm, the answer isn't more
volume — it's an alarm you can't switch off on autopilot.
