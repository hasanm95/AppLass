**Opal is free to download and has a real free tier — but the thing most people install it for is
not in it.** The free plan gives you app blocking with **one rule**, basic timers and your daily Opal
Score. The locked session you cannot end early, and more than one rule, are Opal Pro.

That is the short answer. The longer one is about the price, where the published numbers do not
agree with each other.

## What the free plan includes

| | Free | Pro |
|---|---|---|
| Blocking rules | **One** — a schedule, a time limit or an open limit | Multiple |
| Session timers | Basic | Full |
| Opal Score | Today only | History and trends |
| Session you can't end early | **No** | Yes |
| Price | $0 | See below |

One rule is less limiting than it sounds if your problem is narrow — blocking Instagram and TikTok
every weekday from 9 to 5 is one rule. It becomes limiting the moment you want a different rule for
evenings, or a separate one for weekends.

## Why nobody agrees on what Pro costs

This is the part worth being careful about, and the reason most pages answering this question are
unreliable.

| Source | Quoted price |
|---|---|
| Several review blogs | ~$99.99/year, or $399 lifetime |
| A competitor's blog, checked Sept 2026 | $4.99/month, $19.99/year, or $29.99 once |
| Another guide | $9.99/month |
| **Google Play listing** | **In-app purchases $2.99–$79.99 per item** |

Those cannot all be true at once. Some of the gap is real — app pricing varies by country, by
platform, and by whatever promotion is running — and some of it is simply stale copy. Note also that
most of the sites quoting confident figures sell a competing blocker.

**So: open the app and look.** The price shown in-app, in your currency, on your platform, is the
only number that applies to you. Treat every published figure, including the ones in that table, as a
range rather than a quote.

## Is Opal still free, or did that change?

The free tier still exists. What moves is its contents, which is why people search "is Opal not free
anymore" — it usually means a feature they relied on moved behind Pro, not that the free plan was
withdrawn.

One documented change goes the other way: reviews report Opal **removed ads from the Android free
tier in June 2026**.

## On Android specifically

Opal has been on Google Play since mid-2025, needs Android 10 or newer, and has 500K+ downloads. It
blocks through Android's **AccessibilityService API**, because Android has no equivalent of Apple's
Screen Time API. In practice that makes blocking slightly less absolute than on an iPhone — the
trade-off every Android blocker makes, not an Opal flaw.
