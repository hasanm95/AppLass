**Opal is free to download, and the free plan is more generous than most pages answering this
question admit.** You get **three blocking rules**, app blocking, basic timers and soundscapes, and
today's Opal Score, on iOS, Android and Mac. What you do not get is **Hard Mode** — the session you
cannot turn off — which is the thing most people eventually want.

That is the short answer. The longer one is about the price, where almost every page gets it wrong.

## What the free plan includes

| | Free, forever | Opal PRO |
|---|---|---|
| Blocking rules | **Three** — each a schedule, time limit or open limit | Unlimited |
| App blocking | Yes | Yes |
| Timers and soundscapes | Basic | Advanced |
| Opal Score | Today only | With history |
| Allow Only lists | No | Yes |
| **Hard Mode** — can't turn Opal off | **No** | **Yes** |
| Platforms | iOS, Android, Mac | Same |
| Price | $0 | See below |

Three rules covers more than it sounds like: a weekday work block, an evening block and a separate
weekend rule is three. The free tier stops being enough when you want a fourth, or when you discover
that being able to switch the block off is the whole problem.

## What Opal Pro actually costs

Opal publishes this on its own pricing page. It is not ambiguous, and it is not hard to find:

| Plan | Price |
|---|---|
| Free, forever | **$0** |
| PRO, monthly | **$19.99/month** |
| PRO, yearly | **$99.99/year**, which Opal shows as about $8.29/month |
| PRO, lifetime | A one-time option is offered; third-party reviews report $399 |

Read off [Opal's pricing page](https://opalapp.com/pricing) on 14 November 2026. Opal offers a free
week on Pro. Prices vary by country and by whatever promotion is running, and the lifetime figure is
the one number here we are taking from reviews rather than from Opal.

## Why so many pages get this wrong

Worth knowing, because it tells you how much to trust anything else you read on this question.

Search for Opal's price and you will find confident, contradictory answers: $4.99 a month, $19.99 a
year, $9.99 a month. Google Play adds its own noise by listing in-app purchases as "$2.99 to $79.99
per item", which is a range of purchase tiers rather than a subscription price.

Two things are going on. Most of these pages are **copying each other rather than the source**, so
one stale number propagates for years. And a large share of them are published by companies selling
a competing blocker, which is not a reason to assume bad faith but is a reason to check.

The fix is unglamorous: Opal's pricing page is public. So is the price inside the app, in your
currency, which is the one that actually applies to you.

## Is Opal still free, or did that change?

The free tier still exists. What moves is its contents, which is why people search "is Opal not free
anymore" — it usually means a feature they relied on moved behind Pro, not that the free plan was
withdrawn.

One documented change goes the other way: reviews report Opal **removed ads from the Android free
tier in June 2026**.

## On Android specifically

Opal has been on Google Play since mid-2025, needs Android 10 or newer, and has 500K+ downloads. It
blocks through Android's **AccessibilityService API**, because Android has no equivalent of Apple's
Screen Time API. In practice that makes blocking slightly less absolute than on an iPhone — the
trade-off every Android blocker makes, not an Opal flaw.

More on what that trade-off means day to day in [Opal on Android](/blog/opal-on-android-what-works),
