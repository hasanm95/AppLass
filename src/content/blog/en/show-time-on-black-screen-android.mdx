**To see the time at night without a bright screen, turn on Always On Display.** It shows the time in
small, dim text while the phone is locked. If you are also playing audio — a podcast, rain sounds, a
video you fall asleep to — locking the phone may stop it. In that case keep the app open behind a
black screen that shows only the time.

## Option 1: Always On Display

Most Android phones with an AMOLED screen can keep a small clock on when the phone is locked:

- **Pixel:** Settings → Display → Lock screen → **Always show time and info**.
- **Samsung:** Settings → Lock screen → **Always On Display**. Then pick when it shows: **Tap to show**,
  **Show always**, **Show as scheduled** (for example only at night) or **Show for new notifications**
  ([Samsung support](https://www.samsung.com/uk/support/mobile-devices/how-do-i-enable-always-on-display/)).
- **Other phones:** search Settings for **"Always on"**.

Two things often stop it from appearing: **power saving mode**, which switches Always On Display off on
Samsung phones, and the **Tap to show** setting, which only shows the clock after a tap.

On AMOLED and OLED screens only the lit pixels use power, so the battery cost is small. Over a whole
night it still adds up a little; some people switch it on only while charging.

## The catch: it only works when the phone is locked

Always On Display appears once you lock the phone. That's fine for a silent nightstand. It's a problem
if you fall asleep to something that's playing: many video apps pause as soon as you lock the phone —
YouTube without Premium is the best-known example — and browsers usually do too.

So you end up with a choice: a bright screen with the video playing, or a dark screen with the video
stopped.

## Option 2: A black screen that keeps playing and shows the time

[ScreenVeil](/apps/screenveil) solves this by never locking the phone. It draws a pure black layer over
the screen while the app underneath keeps playing, and it can keep the **time and date** visible on the
black screen:

1. Install ScreenVeil, tap **Start Service** and allow **"display over other apps"**.
2. Start your podcast, sounds or video.
3. Tap the floating button or the Quick Settings tile. The screen turns black, with the time and date
   still visible.
4. To get back, touch the screen and tap unlock.

While the screen is black, touches are blocked, so a sleepy hand can't pause or skip anything. On
AMOLED screens the black pixels are switched off, so the screen gives off almost no light. A **sleep
timer** can also black out the screen on its own after the time you set.

## Which one to use

| At night you want to… | Best option |
|---|---|
| Check the time, nothing playing | Always On Display |
| Fall asleep to audio that keeps playing when locked | Always On Display |
| Fall asleep to a video or app that pauses when locked | A black screen overlay with the clock |
| No light at all | A black screen overlay, clock hidden |

## Tips for a darker bedroom

- **Turn the brightness down before bed**, so if you do unlock, it's not a flash of light.
- **Use a warm screen at night** with Night Light (Eye comfort shield on Samsung).
- **Put the phone face-down** if you don't need the time; no light beats dim light.
- **Charge it out of reach** if you tend to pick it up at 2 a.m.

## The bottom line

Always On Display is the simplest way to keep the time visible at night. When something has to keep
playing and the app stops on lock, a black screen with a clock gives you the time, the sound and an
almost dark room.
