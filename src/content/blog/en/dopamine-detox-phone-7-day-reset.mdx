If a 20-minute podcast feels unbearably slow lately, or you've caught yourself reaching for your
phone mid-conversation without deciding to, that's not a discipline problem — it's a baseline
problem. Months of short-form video reset what your brain considers "normal" stimulation, and
everything below that bar starts to feel boring by comparison.

A dopamine detox doesn't mean giving up your phone. It means spending a week resetting that
baseline — specifically by removing the highest-stimulation feeds — so ordinary life stops feeling
like it's in slow motion.

---

## Why "just delete the apps" usually fails

The default advice is to delete Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube for a week. It sounds clean, but in
practice:

- You need the apps for something legitimate — a DM, a work tutorial, a family group chat
- The moment you reinstall to handle that one thing, the feed is right there again
- Most people are back to their old habits within 2-3 days, having "failed" a detox that was never
  built to survive real life

As [Why I Stopped Fighting My Own Brain](/blog/reclaim-focus-story) puts it, the problem isn't
willpower — it's running "legacy code" against algorithms built to win. Deleting and reinstalling
apps is exactly the kind of manual willpower fight that loses.

## The 7-day reset

This plan keeps your apps installed and usable. It only removes the short-form feed — the specific
mechanism responsible for the dopamine loop — using a Reel Guard rather than an all-or-nothing
app deletion.

**Day 1 — Set the block, not a promise.** Install a Reel Guard and block Instagram Reels, YouTube
Shorts, TikTok, Facebook Reels, and Snapchat Spotlight completely for the week. Don't rely on
willpower to "just not open them" — set the tool so there's nothing to negotiate with later.

**Day 2 — Expect friction, not motivation.** The novelty of "starting a challenge" is gone and the
urge to check feeds is still strong. This is normal. Notice how often your thumb moves before you
consciously decide to check something — that's the reflex [Monk Mode 2.0](/blog/monk-mode-willpower)
describes, firing faster than your actual intent.

**Day 3-4 — The hardest stretch.** Most people describe this as the low point: boredom feels
sharper, and slower activities (reading, a plain walk) feel unusually hard to sit through. This is
the baseline actually shifting, not a sign the detox isn't working.

**Day 5 — The shift starts.** A conversation, a walk, or a book starts to feel less like something
to escape from. This isn't willpower improving — it's your baseline recalibrating now that the
highest-stimulation input has been off for several days.

**Day 6-7 — Notice what changed.** Pay attention to whether you reach for your phone as often in
idle moments, and whether slower activities feel less like a chore. Most people report this is
where the shift becomes obvious rather than subtle.

## After day 7: don't go back to zero

A full week off short-form video resets your baseline, but going back to unlimited Reels on day 8
undoes it within days. The sustainable move is switching from a full block to a **daily and
per-session limit** — enough access to stay connected, not enough to rebuild the old loop. Our guide
on the [session-limit method](/blog/limit-reels-per-day-android) covers exactly how to set that up
once your detox week ends.
