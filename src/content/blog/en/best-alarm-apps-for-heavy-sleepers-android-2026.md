**Loudness is not what wakes a heavy sleeper — a dismiss task is.** If you can turn off a 100-decibel
alarm and remember nothing about it, a louder tone will not fix that. What changes the outcome is an
app that will not stop ringing until you have done something that requires you to be genuinely awake.

**Quick answer:** **Alarmy** is the safest default (50M+ downloads, 4.7 stars, the widest mission set).
**Alarm Clock Xtreme** is the best free puzzle alarm at scale. **AMdroid** has the most unusual
dismissal options, including NFC tags. **Sleep as Android** is best if you want sleep tracking too.
**Arisely** is the only one with a camera-verified push-up mission — but it is a brand-new app, and we
say so plainly below.

## Why heavy sleepers sleep through alarms

It is worth being precise about the failure, because it decides which feature matters.

Dismissing an alarm is a **reflex**. It requires locating a phone and tapping a large button — something
a sleeping brain can execute without ever reaching meaningful consciousness. This is why people
genuinely do not remember turning off alarms: the action completed before awareness did.

Layered on top is **sleep inertia**, the grogginess in the minutes after waking, which is at its worst
when you are woken from deep sleep — exactly where a heavy sleeper spends more of the night. During
inertia, judgment and self-control are measurably impaired, which is the worst possible moment to ask
someone to decide to get up.

So the two things that actually work are: **removing the one-tap dismiss**, and **forcing movement or
cognition** before the alarm stops. Volume helps you hear it. Neither of those is volume.

## The 5 features that decide whether you get up

1. **Exact-time alarms.** If Android's battery optimisation can delay the alarm, nothing else matters.
   It must fire on time with the phone locked and the app closed.
2. **Alarm-channel volume.** It must play on Android's dedicated alarm audio channel, so it stays loud
   through muted media and Do Not Disturb.
3. **Dismiss-by-task missions.** The single biggest failure point is the one-tap off. A task — maths,
   typing, steps, a barcode across the room, push-ups — defeats the reflex. Chaining several in a row
   is stronger still.
4. **Snooze control.** The ability to cap snoozes or disable them. Snoozing fragments your last sleep
   cycle and usually deepens inertia.
5. **No account, local data.** An alarm clock has no reason to need an email address.

## Alarmy — the default pick

**50M+ downloads · 4.7 stars · free with ads + Premium**

Alarmy is the app most people mean when they say "an alarm you can't turn off," and it earns that.
The mission set is the broadest here — photo of a specific place, barcode, maths, memory, typing,
shake and squat — and its **Wake Up Check** restarts the missions if you don't confirm you're awake
shortly after dismissing, which closes the "dismiss then fall back asleep" loophole that defeats most
alarms.

- **Best for:** most heavy sleepers. If you want one app that works and don't want to think about it,
  this is it.
- **Trade-off:** the Play listing marks it as containing ads, Wake Up Check and some sounds are
  Premium, and it asks for a long permission list for an alarm clock — location, camera, device admin
  and accessibility, all optional.

## Alarm Clock Xtreme — best free puzzles at scale

**50M+ downloads · 4.5 stars · free with ads**

Over a million reviews and a genuinely useful free tier. Its puzzles — maths with five difficulty
levels, retyping a password, or scanning a QR code you printed and left across the room — trigger only
when you hit dismiss. It also lets you **cap snoozes** and shorten the snooze interval, which matters
more than it sounds.

- **Best for:** a free, proven mission alarm from a large developer.
- **Trade-off:** ad-supported, and no real physical mission beyond walking to a QR code.

## AMdroid — best for unusual dismissals

**5M+ downloads · 4.3 stars · free with ads + Premium**

AMdroid has the most varied ways to stop an alarm: maths, CAPTCHA, connecting to a specific **Wi-Fi**
network, tapping an **NFC tag**, scanning a barcode, or detecting light. An NFC tag stuck to the
kettle is one of the most effective get-out-of-bed tricks available, because the alarm cannot stop
until you are physically in the kitchen.

- **Best for:** tinkerers who want a hardware-based dismissal.
- **Trade-off:** smaller user base, lowest rating in this table, ad-supported.

## Sleep as Android — best if you want sleep tracking

**10M+ downloads · 4.6 stars · 7-day trial then freemium**

A sleep tracker first, an alarm second. It tracks sleep with the phone's sensors or a wearable, wakes
you during a lighter phase within a window you set, and offers CAPTCHA tasks to dismiss. Integrates
with Spotify, Wear OS and smart lights.

- **Best for:** people who want to understand their sleep, not just survive the morning.
- **Trade-off:** deep and settings-heavy. The listing shows in-app purchases up to $75.

## Arisely — the camera-verified push-up mission

**100+ downloads · not yet rated · free with one banner ad + Pro**

Being straight about this: Arisely is **new**. It has 100+ downloads and no aggregate rating, against
Alarmy's 50M and 4.7 stars. If you want a proven app, pick one of the four above. We make it, so treat
this section accordingly.

What it has that the others don't is an **AI push-up mission**: you set a rep count, and the camera
counts reps and checks form in real time, so the alarm keeps ringing until the set is done. It also
**chains missions** — maths, then a barcode, then push-ups, each with its own difficulty — which is
considerably harder to beat half-asleep than any single task. Two optional guards close the usual
dodges while the alarm rings: one snaps the volume back up if you turn it down, the other blocks the
power-off menu.

It needs **no account**, and alarms, settings and the sleep log stay on the phone. The free version
shows one banner on the Alarms, Sleep and Settings tabs — never on the ring screen or during a
mission.

- **Best for:** heavy sleepers who specifically want a physical, verified wake-up task, and who don't
  mind a new app.
- **Trade-off:** new, few users, no sleep-cycle tracking, and the free tier has a banner ad. Mission

The pattern worth internalising: **maths wakes your brain for ten seconds; walking to the kitchen wakes
your body.** If you have beaten puzzle alarms before by solving them in your sleep, go straight to a
physical mission.

## Before you blame the app

A large share of "my alarm didn't wake me" cases are not about the app at all — the alarm never fired
properly. On Android 12 and later, exact alarm permissions and manufacturer battery optimisation on
Samsung, Xiaomi and OnePlus can delay or kill an alarm in the background.

If your alarms have ever been silent or late, fix that first: our guide to
[why Android alarms don't go off](/blog/why-android-alarms-dont-go-off-exact-alarms) covers the exact
alarm permission and the per-manufacturer battery settings. No mission helps an alarm that never rang.
