**There is no single best AppBlock alternative, because people leave AppBlock for three quite
different reasons** — and each one points somewhere else entirely.

Most lists on this topic rank apps by feature count without asking what went wrong. That produces a
recommendation that is right for maybe a third of readers. This one starts with the reason.

---

## Why people look for an AppBlock alternative

**Three reasons account for nearly all of it: the subscription, the free-tier ceiling, and being
locked out by Strict Mode.** They are not the same problem and they do not share a solution.

| Why you're leaving | What you actually need | Best match |
|---|---|---|
| Don't want a subscription | Genuinely free, not free-trial | Mindful Guard, ScreenZen, Digital Wellbeing |
| Hit the free schedule limit | Same breadth, different pricing | Stay Focused |
| Locked out by Strict Mode | An exit, then something gentler | Safe mode, then ScreenZen |
| Blocks feel too harsh | Friction, not walls | ScreenZen, One Sec |
| Only care about Reels/Shorts | Feed-level blocking, nothing else | Mindful Guard |
| Need laptop coverage too | Cross-device sync | Freedom |

AppBlock Premium is **$4.99/month, $29.99/year, or $89.99 lifetime**, after a seven-day trial. The
free tier works but caps how many schedules you can build and leaves out backup. That cap is the
single most common trigger for switching — people build a setup they like, hit the ceiling, and go
looking rather than pay.

## What AppBlock genuinely does better

Worth saying plainly, because a comparison that pretends the competitor is bad is not useful:

- **Website blocking**, which most Android-native blockers do not attempt.
- **Multiple scheduling profiles** for different parts of the day, which is the feature people miss
  most after leaving.
- **In-app element blocking**, including Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts specifically — the same
  ground Mindful Guard occupies.
- **Backup and sync**, so a setup survives a new phone.
- **Usage statistics** detailed enough to act on.

If you use most of that, the honest recommendation is to pay for AppBlock. The lifetime option at
$89.99 works out cheaper than eighteen months of a competitor's subscription, and switching to
recover a feature you actually use is a bad trade.

## Where Mindful Guard fits — and where it does not

Mindful Guard is narrower on purpose. It blocks short-form video feeds — Reels, Shorts, TikTok,
Facebook, Snapchat — with daily and per-session limits, and does not try to be a scheduling suite.

**What it does that AppBlock does not:**

- **Free with no premium tier.** Not a trial, not a schedule cap.
- **No account, ever.** Nothing to sign into.
- **Fully offline**, with a verified "No data collected" badge on Play.

**What AppBlock does that it does not:** website blocking, profile scheduling, backup, statistics.

On privacy specifically, be fair to AppBlock: it states that it collects no personal information
beyond what its optional Academy login and backup need, both of which you can skip. It does require
your installed-app list to function, as any blocker must. The argument for Mindful Guard is *no
account and no network at all*, not that the alternative is misbehaving.

## The alternatives, briefly

**Stay Focused** — the closest like-for-like replacement. App and website blocking, schedules, usage
and launch limits, profiles, its own strict mode. If you are leaving over price, note that matching
AppBlock's breadth generally means matching its business model too.

**ScreenZen** — free and donation-supported, built around friction rather than walls. It adds a
pause and a prompt before an app opens instead of locking you out. Genuinely the best answer for
people who found AppBlock too punishing, and the right landing spot after a Strict Mode lockout.

**Digital Wellbeing** — already on your phone, free, and enough if you only need app timers and a
Focus Mode. Its weakness is the one-tap extension on every limit, covered in
[Digital Wellbeing vs third-party focus apps](/blog/android-digital-wellbeing-vs-third-party-focus-apps).

**Freedom** — the answer only if your distractions include a laptop. Cross-device sync is its real
differentiator; on Android alone it is not worth the VPN overhead.

**Mindful Guard** — free, offline, no account, focused on short-form feeds.

## If Strict Mode has you locked out right now

This is its own category of visitor, and the alternatives list is not what you need first.

AppBlock's Strict Mode has two levels; the second resists uninstalling until your timer expires. It
cannot actually prevent it — **no Play Store app can**, because Android removed that capability
years ago. In order:

1. Revoke the accessibility permission in Settings → Accessibility.
2. Deactivate device admin under Settings → Security → Device admin apps.
3. Boot into **safe mode**, where third-party apps do not run, and uninstall normally.

The full explanation of why each step works is in
[app blockers that can't be uninstalled](/blog/app-blocker-cannot-be-uninstalled-android).

Then think about what happened before installing a replacement. Getting locked out badly enough to
search for an exit usually means the block was set harder than the habit required. The next app
should be gentler, not stricter.

## Switching without losing your setup

1. **Screenshot your AppBlock profiles** before uninstalling. Free-tier setups have no backup, and
   rebuilding from memory is where most switches stall.
2. **Wait out any active Strict Mode session.** Uninstalling mid-session is the messy path.
3. **Install the replacement first**, configure it, and confirm it survives a reboot.
4. **Then remove AppBlock**, and cancel Premium separately if you subscribed — removing the app does
   not cancel a Play subscription.

That last point catches people out. Check Play Store → Subscriptions.

## Bottom line

If you use AppBlock's scheduling profiles and website blocking, pay for it — nothing free matches
that breadth, and the lifetime price is reasonable for what it is.

If you hit the paywall and realised you only ever blocked Reels and Shorts, you were paying for a
suite to use one feature, and a free focused tool is the better fit. And if Strict Mode locked you
out, deal with the exit first and pick something with less force next time.
