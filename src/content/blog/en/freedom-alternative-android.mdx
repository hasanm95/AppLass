Freedom is one of the most established names in distraction blocking, and it earns that reputation
honestly — 4.31 stars across roughly 7,500 ratings and 1.9 million downloads on Android alone. But
its architecture was built for syncing your phone, Mac, and Windows PC together, and that design
carries real costs on Android specifically: a VPN tunnel that affects battery, an account you have
to create, and a free tier that caps you at 2-hour sessions with no recurring schedule.

If you only need to control your Android phone — and you'd rather not hand over an email, an
internet connection, or your battery life to do it — here's what to look for in an alternative, and
where each option actually wins.

---

## Where Freedom's Android app costs you something

**It runs on a VPN.** Freedom's mobile blocking ("Freedom Profile") works by routing your traffic
through a local VPN tunnel and filtering it there. That's effective, but keeping a VPN tunnel active
has a real cost: user reports describe the phone heating up and battery draining noticeably faster
during sessions, and connections becoming unstable the longer a session runs — particularly on
older devices or phones with aggressive battery management.

**It requires an account.** To use Freedom at all, you sign up with an email and password, and your
sessions sync through Freedom's servers so your devices stay in step. That's useful if you're
blocking across a laptop and a phone at once — but it also means your usage data passes through a
server you don't control, for a feature (cross-device sync) you may not need.

**The free plan is capped hard.** Freedom's free tier allows unlimited devices and custom blocklists,
but sessions are "Start Now" only, capped at 2 hours, with no recurring sessions and no advance
scheduling — and Locked Mode (the feature that stops you from quitting mid-session) is Premium-only
at $39.99/year (or a $99.50 lifetime option).

## A native, offline alternative built for Android

MindfulGuard takes a different approach specifically because it's built for Android only, with no
cross-platform ambitions to design around:

- **No VPN.** Blocking runs through Android's native Accessibility Service — the same category of
  system hook Android itself uses for Digital Wellbeing — so there's no tunnel filtering your
  traffic and no measurable battery cost. In testing on a Pixel 8 Pro, native blocking added under
  1% daily battery drain.
- **No account, no internet.** Nothing syncs to a server because nothing needs to. Every block,
  schedule, and limit is stored and enforced entirely on-device.
- **No session cap on the free tier.** Scheduled blocks and an unbreakable Strict Mode are available
  free, with premium unlocking further customization from $3.99/month.
- **Reel Guard included.** Beyond whole-app blocking, MindfulGuard also targets the short-form feed
  specifically inside Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, and Snapchat — something Freedom's
  whole-app-or-nothing model doesn't offer. Our
