screen with the video in front, or use NewPipe. The third is Premium Lite, which added background

**If you came here because the Firefox add-on stopped working:** run the 4-step checklist in the next
section. It brings the add-on back for some accounts. If it does not, the add-on is no longer the
problem — YouTube is pausing playback on its side, and no Firefox setting changes that.

## Firefox add-on stopped working? Run this checklist first

[Video Background Play Fix](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/video-background-play-fix/)
is a Firefox for Android add-on (about 229,000 users) that blocks the Page Visibility API and the
Fullscreen API, so YouTube is never told the tab went into the background. Its last update, version
1.8.1, shipped in November 2024 — before YouTube's current enforcement existed. It still works on some
accounts, because YouTube's change did not reach every account at once.

Go through these in order:

1. **Update Firefox.** The add-on needs Firefox 82 or newer for Android, and old builds behave
   differently. Update from the Play Store.
2. **Check the add-on is enabled.** Firefox menu → Add-ons → Video Background Play Fix → enabled. If
   you use private tabs, also turn on "Allow in private browsing."
3. **Reload YouTube after enabling it.** The add-on only takes effect on pages loaded after it is on.
4. **Test it.** Play a video in Firefox, press Home or lock the screen, and wait a few seconds. If
   audio stops within seconds every time, YouTube is enforcing the block on your account.

Some users also report success with a user-agent switcher, per
[Tech Advisor](https://www.techadvisor.com/article/3051828/youtube-blocks-background-playback-in-mobile-browsers.html).
That is the same game of whack-a-mole: it works until YouTube notices.

**If the checklist does not bring it back, stop troubleshooting Firefox.** Use one of the three
methods below instead — they do not depend on YouTube failing to notice anything.

## Fix 1 — Keep the video in front and black out the screen (free, ~30 seconds)

The most reliable free option sidesteps background play entirely. **Leave the video playing in the
YouTube app, in the foreground, and cover the screen with a black overlay.** YouTube is never sent to
the background, so there is nothing for it to pause.

[ScreenVeil](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.screenveil.app) is a free Android app
that does exactly this:

1. Install ScreenVeil and tap **Start Service**. Grant the "display over other apps" permission, the
   only one it needs.
2. Start your video in the YouTube app.
3. Tap ScreenVeil's floating button. The screen turns black and blocks touches, so a pocket or a sleeve
   cannot pause or skip anything.
4. Touch the screen to show the unlock button when you want the picture back.

Be clear about what this is: the screen is **still on**, just showing black. That is the point,
because YouTube never sees it turn off. It also means that on an LCD phone the backlight stays lit, so
you save little power. On an **AMOLED or OLED** phone, black pixels are switched off, so display power
drops substantially. How much depends on brightness; we went through the measured
numbers in [does dark mode save battery on AMOLED](/blog/does-dark-mode-save-battery-amoled).

It works with any app that plays audio, not just YouTube, and it has 10,000+ downloads on Google Play.

## Fix 2 — NewPipe (free, true screen-off playback)

[NewPipe](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/org.schabi.newpipe/) is an open-source YouTube client. It
does not use YouTube's own player, so YouTube's Premium check does not apply to it, and it supports
genuine background play with the screen off.

**What you get:**
- True background play, including with the screen locked
- No ads
- Downloads for offline listening
- No Google account required

**The catches:**
- It is on [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/org.schabi.newpipe/), not the Play Store. You
  allow installs from one extra source, install F-Droid, then install NewPipe through it.
- It does not sign in to your Google account, so no synced subscriptions, history or playlists. You
  can import subscriptions manually.
- Because it works independently of YouTube's player, it **occasionally breaks** after YouTube
  changes something. Fixes usually arrive within days through an update.

**Best for:** people who want real screen-off playback for free and do not mind a separate app.

## Fix 3 — YouTube Premium Lite (now includes background play)

Premium Lite launched without background play. That changed on
[February 24, 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/02/24/youtube-premium-lite-background-play-downloads/),
when YouTube added background play and downloads to Lite at no extra cost. In the US it costs
**$7.99 a month**.

What Lite covers and what it does not:

| | Premium Lite | Premium |
|---|---|---|
| Price (US) | $7.99/month | $13.99/month |
| Ad-free videos | Most videos | All videos |
| Background play | Yes, except music videos and Shorts | Yes, everything |
| Downloads | Yes, except music videos and Shorts | Yes |
| YouTube Music | No | Included |

If what you want in the background is podcasts, lectures or long videos, Lite covers it. If it is
music, Lite does not, and full Premium or YouTube Music is the paid route.

## Why it broke: what changed in January 2026

Reports of background playback stopping in third-party browsers began around January 12, 2026, first
from Brave users, then from Samsung Internet, Vivaldi and Edge. By the end of the month Google had
confirmed it to [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-background-play-broken-3636179/)
and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/02/02/youtube-background-playback-workarounds-not-working-third-party-browsers/).
A YouTube spokesperson said background playback is "a feature intended to be exclusive for YouTube
Premium members."

The practical effect: when a free account is playing YouTube in a mobile browser and you switch apps
or turn the screen off, YouTube pauses playback. It does this whatever the browser reports, which is
why browser settings and the old "desktop site" trick no longer help — and why the only durable
methods are the three above, which do not pretend the browser is still in focus.
