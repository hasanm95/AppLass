## Why "just block YouTube" is bad advice

A whole-app blocker treats YouTube as a single on/off switch. Block it during focus hours and you
also lose:

- The tutorial you needed for work
- Your subscriptions and watch-later queue
- Podcasts and long-form video you chose to watch

That's why whole-app blocks on YouTube rarely survive more than a few days — the cost is too high,
so people disable the blocker the first time they need a real video. The fix isn't giving up
YouTube. It's targeting the one feed inside it that's built to be infinite.

## Daily limit vs. per-session limit

Two settings do the actual work, and you can use either or both:

- **Daily limit** — a total Shorts budget per day (e.g. 10 minutes). Once it's spent, Shorts is
  blocked until the next day — the rest of YouTube stays open.
- **Per-session limit** — a cap on any single sitting (e.g. 3 minutes). This is what stops the
  scroll before it becomes a session, since the damage is rarely opening Shorts once — it's the
  half hour you didn't plan to lose.

Set a hard limit and there's nothing to negotiate with. No "add 5 more minutes" button to tap.
