**Snapchat has no switch to turn Spotlight off.** The feed sits in the bottom bar, one tap from your
chats. Android's app timer can stop it, but only by locking you out of Snapchat entirely. To keep
Snaps and Chat and lose only the endless feed, you need a limit that works on the Spotlight screen
itself.

## Why Spotlight is hard to avoid

Most people keep Snapchat for messaging. Spotlight is Snapchat's short-video feed, and it is built
the same way as Reels and Shorts: full-screen, auto-playing and endless. Because it lives next to
the camera and Chat, you open it out of habit while you were really just replying to a friend.

Snap is pushing it hard. In its Q2 2025 results, Snap said Spotlight reached **more than 550 million
monthly users** and accounts for **more than 40% of content time spent** in Snapchat
([Snap Q2 2025 results](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2025/q2/Q2-25-Final-Earnings-Release.pdf)).

That is the problem with "just don't open it." The app you need and the feed you want to avoid are
the same app.

## What Snapchat lets you change

Snapchat doesn't offer a setting to hide Spotlight, but you can take away most of its pull:

1. **Turn off Spotlight notifications.** Profile → settings gear → **Notifications**, then switch off
   the Spotlight-related options. On some versions they are labelled **Trending Public Content**.
2. **Keep your Snaps out of Spotlight.** When you send a Snap, don't pick Spotlight on the Send To
   screen. With nothing posted there, you have no views to keep checking.
3. **Remove a Snap you already posted.** Open your profile, find your Spotlight Snaps and delete the
   ones you don't want public anymore.
4. **Use Family Center (parents).** It shows a teen's friends and can restrict sensitive content, but
   it does not hide Spotlight or limit time spent on it.

None of these stop the scroll once you are in the feed. For that you need a limit on the feed itself.

## What Android's Digital Wellbeing does, and where it falls short

Android's **Digital Wellbeing** (Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls → Dashboard) lets you
set an **app timer** on Snapchat. When the timer runs out, the app icon greys out until midnight.

Two problems:

1. **It blocks everything.** Chat, Snaps, Stories and the camera stop along with Spotlight.
2. **It is easy to undo.** The timer is a few taps away in Settings, so in a weak moment you just
   extend it.

## How to limit only the Spotlight feed on Android

A feed-level limit closes Spotlight and leaves the rest of Snapchat alone. With
[Mindful Guard](/apps/mindful-guard):

1. Install Mindful Guard and allow the **Accessibility Service** when asked. It uses it to notice
   when the Spotlight feed opens.
2. Open **Reel Guard** and turn on **Snapchat Spotlight**.
3. Set a **daily limit**, a **per-session limit**, or both.
4. Optionally add a cooldown between sessions so you can't start another one right away.

When you hit the limit, Spotlight closes. Chat, Snaps, Stories and the camera keep working, so you
can still reply to the friend who messaged you.

## A limit that's easy to live with

A zero-minute limit tends to get switched off within days. Start with something you can keep:

| Goal | Daily limit | Per-session limit |
|---|---|---|
| Cut back gently | 20 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Mostly quit | 5 minutes | 2 minutes |
| Quit completely | 0 minutes | — |

The per-session limit does most of the work. A daily cap alone can be spent in one long scroll; a
session cap breaks that scroll early.

## Do the same for your other feeds

If you only limit Spotlight, the habit tends to move to the next feed. Reel Guard can apply the same
daily and per-session limits to Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Facebook Reels. See
[how to limit Reels per day](/blog/limit-reels-per-day-android) and
[how to block the TikTok feed](/blog/how-to-block-tiktok-feed-android).

## The bottom line

Snapchat won't let you turn Spotlight off, and Android's app timer takes your chats with it. A
feed-level limit is the one option that caps Spotlight and leaves the parts of Snapchat you actually
use.
