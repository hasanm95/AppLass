**A blocker you can switch off in four taps is not a blocker.** Settings, App Info, force stop,
done — and the block you set up on Sunday is gone by Tuesday afternoon. Strict Mode closes those
four taps: while a session runs there is no override button, the app's own settings screens send
you back, and leaving early costs a 20-minute wait with blocking still on.

What it is not is a wall. No Android app can be one, and this page is going to be specific about
why.

## What Strict Mode actually does

While a strict session is running:

- **Opening Mindful Guard's App Info sends you straight back.** That is the force-stop route, and
  it is the one almost everyone reaches for first.
- **Opening its Accessibility settings sends you back.** Revoking the permission is the other quick
  kill, because the blocker needs it to work.
- **Opening its uninstall screen sends you back.**
- **There is no "open anyway" button.** Not a confirmation, not a five-second countdown, not a
  "just this once". The absence is the point — every dialogue is a moment where a half-decided tap
  gets through.
- **Leaving early means tapping Unlock early and waiting 20 minutes**, with the block still running
  for the whole wait.

It is opt-in, gated behind a consent sheet, and shows an active badge while it runs. You can turn it
on from Home for a single session or per schedule, with the lock icon in the schedule list.

## Disabling a blocker and uninstalling it are two different problems

Most writing on this subject collapses them, which is why most of it is wrong.

**Uninstalling** is the dramatic one, and it is the one Android settled years ago: an ordinary app
cannot prevent its own removal. **Disabling** is the one that actually happens — force-stopping the
app, or revoking the accessibility permission so it stops seeing anything. It takes seconds, it
leaves the app installed, and you can tell yourself it does not count.

Strict Mode is aimed at the second problem, because the second problem is the one you have at 11pm.
The honest, longer answer about the first lives in
[app blockers that can't be uninstalled](/blog/app-blocker-cannot-be-uninstalled-android).

## How to turn it on

1. Install [Mindful Guard](/apps/mindful-guard) and allow the **Accessibility Service** when asked.
2. **Set up the block you actually want first** — apps, schedule, and
   [websites](/blog/how-to-block-websites-on-android-chrome), since a block that stops at apps
   leaves the browser wide open. Strict Mode locks whatever is running, so get it right before you
   lock it.
3. Turn on **Strict Mode** from the Home toggle, or the lock icon on a specific schedule. A consent
   sheet explains what you are agreeing to first.
4. Start the session. The active badge confirms it is running.
5. If you genuinely need out, tap **Unlock early** and wait 20 minutes.

The first strict session is free, so you can feel what it is like before deciding it is worth
paying for. After that it is a Premium feature.

## What Strict Mode cannot stop

This is the section most vendors leave out, and leaving it out is how people end up trusting a tool
at exactly the wrong moment.

**Safe Mode.** Android can boot with third-party apps disabled. A blocker is a third-party app, so
in Safe Mode it is not running. Nothing on the Play Store can change this.

**A factory reset.** Wipes the device, and the blocker with it.

**A second user profile, or a spare phone.** The block lives on one profile of one device.

**Uninstalling, eventually.** Strict Mode makes it slow. It cannot make it impossible, because
Android deliberately keeps the device owner in final control — the same design decision that stops
genuinely malicious apps from entrenching themselves. You benefit from that rule far more often than
it inconveniences you.

So the honest claim is narrow: **Strict Mode removes the reflexive exits and leaves only deliberate
ones.** Anyone selling you more than that is selling you something Android does not permit.

## Why a 20-minute wait beats a wall

A wall is the obvious design, and it is the wrong one. Hard walls get uninstalled — in frustration,
on the day you genuinely needed the app, and then they never get set up again.

What the 20 minutes exploits is that the urge and the decision have different lifespans. The pull
toward a feed is intense and brief; wanting to use your phone less is mild and durable. A wait that
outlasts the first and defers to the second ends, most of the time, with you not bothering. Nothing
was forbidden. You just were not interested any more by the time the door opened.

That is also why the wait runs *with blocking still on*. A countdown you can watch while scrolling is
not a wait, it is a queue.

It is the same logic as
[negotiated reel extensions](/blog/limit-reels-per-day-android), where each extra costs more than the
last, and the same reason [a pause screen](/blog/best-app-to-block-social-media-on-android-in-2026)
beats a toast notification.

## If you are blocking something you are trying to quit

A good number of people who search for an unremovable blocker are not trying to read fewer
headlines. They are blocking gambling, or porn, or a specific app that has cost them something.

Two things worth saying plainly.

**A blocker is a real tool.** Putting twenty minutes between an impulse and an action is not
nothing — that gap is where most of the damage gets avoided, and it is the entire reason this
feature exists.

**A blocker is not a treatment.** If an app on your phone is the only thing between you and a
relapse, the setting is not the problem to solve. That is worth taking to a GP or a therapist, and
for gambling specifically, [GamCare](https://www.gamcare.org.uk/) in the UK and
[Gamblers Anonymous](https://www.gamblersanonymous.org/) run free, confidential support. Use the
blocker *and* the help, not the blocker instead of it.

## The bottom line

Strict Mode is a commitment device, not a lock. It removes the four-tap exit, makes the deliberate
exit cost twenty minutes, and is honest that Safe Mode and a factory reset exist. That narrower
promise is the one worth trusting — a tool that claimed to be unbreakable would be lying to you at
the moment you most need it to be straight.

Set the block up properly first, including the websites. Then lock it.
