**A Pomodoro timer tells you when to work; an app blocker makes sure your phone doesn't interrupt.**
Start a 25-minute focus timer that blocks your distracting apps, work until it ends, take a 5-minute
break, repeat. Your phone can't break a pomodoro halfway through, because the apps that would do it
won't open.

## The Pomodoro Technique in 30 seconds

Created by Francesco Cirillo, the method is simple:

1. Pick one task.
2. Work on it for **25 minutes** — one pomodoro.
3. Take a **5-minute break**.
4. After **four pomodoros**, take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

Cirillo developed it in the late 1980s as a university student, using a tomato-shaped kitchen timer,
which is where the name comes from ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomodoro_Technique)). The research behind it is still small, but a
2025 review in *BMC Medical Education* found that timed breaks like Pomodoro improved focus and reduced
mental fatigue compared with self-paced breaks ([BMC Medical Education](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-025-08001-0)).

It works because 25 minutes is short enough to start and long enough to get something done. It fails
for one common reason: the phone.

## Why the timer alone isn't enough

A Pomodoro timer counts down. It doesn't stop a message preview from pulling you into a chat, or your
thumb from opening Instagram by reflex. Ten minutes in, you "just check" something, and the pomodoro
is over in all but name.

Pairing the timer with an app blocker closes that gap: during the focus block, the apps that pull you
away simply don't open.

With [Mindful Guard](/apps/mindful-guard):

5. When the timer ends, the apps open again. Take your break.

## Choosing your lengths

| Situation | Focus block | Break |
|---|---|---|
| Classic Pomodoro | 25 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Deep work, coding, writing | 50 minutes | 10 minutes |
| Hard to get started | 15 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Revising for exams | 25–30 minutes | 5 minutes, longer every 4 rounds |

Choose a length you can finish without touching your phone. Finishing short blocks builds the habit
faster than abandoning long ones.

## Make your breaks real breaks

- **Stand up.** Walk, stretch, refill your water.
- **Skip the endless feeds.** Five minutes of Reels rarely stays five minutes. If that happens, limit
  the feeds as well: [how to limit Reels per day](/blog/limit-reels-per-day-android).
- **Keep the next task ready.** Write down where you stopped, so the next pomodoro starts fast.

## The bottom line

The Pomodoro Technique gives your work a rhythm; an app blocker protects it. Start a 25-minute focus
timer that blocks your distractions with each pomodoro, and the hardest part of the method — not
checking your phone — takes care of itself.
