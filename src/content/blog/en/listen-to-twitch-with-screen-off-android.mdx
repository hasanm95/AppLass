**Start with the Twitch app's own settings.** Turn on background audio in your preferences, and use
**Audio Only** in the player if you just want the sound. If the stream still stops when you lock the
phone — or you're watching in a browser — keep it open behind a black screen that blocks touches.

## Why streams stop when the screen goes off

When you lock an Android phone, the app on screen moves to the background. What happens next depends
on the app:

- **The Twitch app** keeps playing only if its background audio setting allows it.
- **A mobile browser** usually pauses video as soon as the screen locks.
- **Battery savers** on some Samsung, Xiaomi and OnePlus phones can stop apps in the background.

## Step 1: Turn on background audio and Audio Only in Twitch

1. Open Twitch, tap your profile picture and go to your **account settings → preferences**.
2. Find the setting for **background audio** and set it to keep playing (always, or with headphones).
3. In a stream, open the player's **settings (gear)** and choose **Audio Only** if you don't need the
   picture. It also uses much less mobile data.

Menu names move between app versions, so look for the closest match if yours differ.

## Step 2: If it still stops, black out the screen instead

If the stream still pauses, or you watch in a browser, don't lock the phone. Keep the stream open and
cover the screen with black. With [ScreenVeil](/apps/screenveil):

1. Install ScreenVeil, tap **Start Service** and allow **"display over other apps"**. It is the only
   permission you grant.
2. Open the stream and let it play.
3. Tap ScreenVeil's floating button or its Quick Settings tile. The screen turns black; the stream keeps
   playing.
4. To check chat, touch the screen and tap unlock.

Touches are blocked while the screen is black, so your pocket can't tap a clip, an ad or the back
button. Pocket mode can also turn the screen black on its own when the phone goes into your pocket.

## Battery and data

| Method | Screen power | Mobile data |
|---|---|---|
| Watching normally | Highest | Highest |
| Audio Only in Twitch | Screen can be off | Much lower |
| Black screen overlay (AMOLED) | Much lower | Same as watching |
| Black screen overlay (LCD) | Small saving | Same as watching |

On AMOLED and OLED screens a pure black pixel is switched off, so a black overlay uses far less screen
power than a lit stream. On LCD the backlight stays on. For long streams on mobile data, Audio Only is
the better choice when it works for you.

## The bottom line

Turn on background audio and use Audio Only in the Twitch app first. When that isn't enough, keep the
stream open behind a black screen: the audio keeps going, nothing lights up, and stray touches can't
interrupt it. For YouTube, see [YouTube background play in 2026](/blog/youtube-background-play-2026-workaround).
