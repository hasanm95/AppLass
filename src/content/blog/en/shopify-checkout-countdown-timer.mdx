**Only if you are on Shopify Plus.** Shopify's own documentation is unambiguous: checkout UI
extensions for the information, shipping, and payment steps are
[available only to stores on a Shopify Plus plan](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/checkout-ui-extensions).

Every other plan can still extend part of checkout — just not the part where a timer would create
urgency. This is the full picture: what each plan can do, which methods have stopped working, and
why a checkout timer is usually the wrong idea even when it is technically available.

---

## Which surfaces can each plan customise?

**Basic, Shopify, and Advanced plans can extend the Thank You and Order Status pages. Only Plus can
extend the checkout steps themselves.** Shopify opened those two post-purchase pages to non-Plus
plans in a
[May 2024 update](https://shopify.dev/changelog/new-checkout-functionality-for-merchants-on-basic-shopify-and-advanced-plans),
which is the source of a lot of confusion — non-Plus merchants *can* customise "checkout," just not
the steps before payment.

| Surface | Basic / Shopify / Advanced | Plus | Useful for a timer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product page | ✅ | ✅ | Yes, for real flash sales |
| Cart page | ✅ | ✅ | **Yes — the best placement** |
| Cart drawer | ✅ | ✅ | Yes |
| Checkout: information | ❌ | ✅ | Rarely |
| Checkout: shipping | ❌ | ✅ | Rarely |
| Checkout: payment | ❌ | ✅ | Rarely |
| Thank You page | ✅ | ✅ | No — order already placed |
| Order Status page | ✅ | ✅ | No — order already placed |

The Thank You and Order Status pages are genuinely useful surfaces, just not for this. They suit
post-purchase upsells, surveys, referral codes, and download links — anything that assumes the sale
already happened.

## Methods that no longer work

Search results for this question are full of advice that was correct years ago. Two in particular
keep resurfacing:

**Editing `checkout.liquid`.** This stopped working for in-checkout pages on **13 August 2024**, and
it was a Plus-only file before that. Checkout UI extensions replaced it. Any tutorial that says to
paste a timer snippet into `checkout.liquid` is describing something that cannot work on a current
store.

**Shopify Script Editor.** Scripts were Plus-only, they operate on discounts and shipping rather
than rendering interface, and they have been superseded by Shopify Functions. Neither Scripts nor
Functions draw anything on screen — they change what things cost, not what the buyer sees. No
countdown can come from either.

If a vendor tells you they can put a timer in your non-Plus checkout, ask exactly which extension
target they are using. There isn't one.

## Even on Plus, checkout is the wrong place

Here is the part the plan-availability question distracts from. Suppose you are on Plus and the
restriction does not apply to you. **You still probably should not do it.**

A shopper on the payment step has already decided. They are typing a card number. What a deadline
adds at that moment is not motivation but pressure during a task that requires accuracy — and
pressure applied after the decision is the definition of a dark pattern rather than a nudge. It also
sits badly with the top reason people abandon: in
[Baymard's data](/blog/shopify-cart-abandonment-benchmarks-2026), 40% leave because of unexpected
costs, and none of them are helped by a clock.

The decision a timer can actually influence happens one step earlier. At the cart, the shopper is
genuinely choosing between completing now and leaving to think about it. That is where a deadline
changes an outcome instead of just adding stress.

## Where to put the timer instead

Ranked by how much work each placement does:

1. **Cart page and cart drawer.** Available on every plan, and positioned exactly where the
   buy-now-or-drift decision happens. This is where a
   [cart reservation timer](/blog/shopify-cart-reservation-timer) belongs — phrased as a cart that
   expires, since Shopify does not hold stock for you.
2. **Product page**, for genuine time-boxed sales. Real deadline, real event.
3. **Sticky cart bar on mobile.** Pairs a persistent action with the deadline; see the
   [mobile sticky cart guide](/blog/mobile-sticky-cart-guide).
4. **Thank You page**, for a post-purchase offer window — a real use of a timer, just a completely
   different one.

And before adding urgency at all, deal with the reason most carts actually die. A
[free-shipping progress bar](/blog/free-shipping-bar-shopify) addresses a 40% objection; a countdown
addresses none of the top ten.

## Bottom line

The technical answer is short: checkout-step timers need Plus, everyone gets Thank You and Order
Status, and `checkout.liquid` has been dead since August 2024.

The useful answer is that the restriction is saving most merchants from a mistake. Checkout is where
you remove friction, not where you add it. Put the deadline at the cart, where the decision is still
open, and leave the payment step as boring and frictionless as you can make it.
