**YouTube has its own time tools — a break reminder, a bedtime reminder and a Shorts feed limit — but on an
adult account you can dismiss all of them.** Turn them on first; they help. For a limit that holds, set a
Digital Wellbeing app timer for the whole app, or use an app blocker that caps only the Shorts feed or
blocks YouTube on a schedule.

## How much time YouTube takes

YouTube viewing keeps growing. Digital i, which measures viewing across 20 markets, found average daily
YouTube use per account rose from **87 to 99 minutes** between 2024 and 2025
([Digital i](https://www.digital-i.com/news-articles/press-release-youtube-overtakes-netflix-for-average-daily-viewing-time-while-netflix-channel-achieves-widest-reach-of-2025)).
On a phone, most of the unplanned part comes from two places: autoplaying recommendations and the endless
Shorts feed.

## Step 1: Turn on YouTube's own time tools

In the YouTube app, tap your **profile picture → Settings**. Depending on your version, the options are
under **Time management** or **General**:

- **Remind me to take a break** — a prompt after a set time, such as every 30 or 60 minutes.
- **Remind me when it's bedtime** — a prompt at a time you choose.
- **Shorts feed limit** — a daily cap on scrolling Shorts, from **15 minutes to 2 hours**
  ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/22/youtube-adds-at-timer-for-you-to-stop-scrolling-shorts)).
  Since 2026 you can also set it to **0 minutes**, which keeps the Shorts feed empty
  ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/youtube-shorts-kill-switch/)).

**The catch:** on an adult account these are reminders. When the Shorts limit is reached, YouTube shows a
prompt you can dismiss and keep scrolling, and the limit only works in the mobile app
([Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/04/youtube-how-to-block-shorts-new-feature-catch.html)).
That's why searches for "Shorts feed limit not working" are common. For supervised teen accounts, the
parent's limit can't be dismissed.

## Step 2: Set a Digital Wellbeing timer for the whole app

To cap YouTube as a whole: **Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls → Dashboard → YouTube →
App timer**. When the time is up, YouTube is paused until midnight.

It covers everything in YouTube — Shorts, normal videos, music — and the timer can be raised again in
Settings whenever you want.

## Compare your options

| Tool | Limits | Can you dismiss it? |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube break / bedtime reminder | A prompt after a set time | Yes |
| YouTube Shorts feed limit (adult) | Daily Shorts time, 0 min to 2 h | Yes |
| YouTube Shorts feed limit (supervised teen) | Daily Shorts time | No |
| Digital Wellbeing app timer | All of YouTube, daily | Easy to raise in Settings |
| App blocker (feed-level) | Only Shorts, daily and per session | No override button |
| App blocker (schedule or timer) | All of YouTube, by time window | No override button |

## Tips that make the limit easier

- **Turn off autoplay** in YouTube's settings, so one video doesn't roll into the next.
- **Watch from Subscriptions**, not the Home feed, when you open the app on purpose.
- **Use a per-session limit for Shorts.** A daily cap alone can be spent in one sitting; see
  [the session method](/blog/limit-reels-per-day-android).
- **Block Shorts entirely if needed:** [how to block YouTube Shorts on Android](/blog/how-to-block-youtube-shorts-android).

## The bottom line

Turn on YouTube's own reminders and Shorts feed limit — they help, but on an adult account they're easy to
dismiss. For a limit that holds, cap the whole app with Digital Wellbeing, or use an app blocker to limit
only Shorts or block YouTube on a schedule with no override button.
