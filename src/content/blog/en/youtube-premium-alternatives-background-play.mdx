**In February 2026 YouTube gave Premium Lite background play and downloads**, and most advice about
Premium alternatives has not caught up. The cheapest official plan now does the one thing people
mainly sideloaded apps to get.

That reframes the question. Free alternatives no longer compete against a missing feature — they
compete against $8.99 a month. Some still win that argument. This is what each one actually
replaces, and what none of them do.

If you only want the setup steps for background playback itself, our
[guide to background play methods that still work](/blog/youtube-background-play-2026-workaround)
covers the mechanics. This post is about whether to pay at all.

---

## What the official tiers cost and include

**Premium Lite is $8.99 per month in the US and now includes background play and downloads for most
non-music videos.** Full Premium is $15.99. Both rose in the June 2026 price increase — Lite went up
from $7.99.

| | Premium Lite ($8.99) | Premium ($15.99) |
|---|---|---|
| Ad-free on most videos | ✅ | ✅ |
| Background play | ✅ since Feb 2026 | ✅ |
| Offline downloads | ✅ since Feb 2026 | ✅ |
| Ad-free on music videos | ❌ | ✅ |
| YouTube Music | ❌ | ✅ |
| Shorts covered | ❌ | ✅ |

The family plan is $26.99 for up to six members, which is the best per-person value by a wide margin
if you have anyone to share with. The student rate is $8.99, and since January 2026 it converts to
full price once you hit the four-year cap or fail re-verification.

**The honest summary of the paid options:** if you listen to music on YouTube, you want full
Premium, because Lite explicitly excludes music content. If you listen to podcasts, lectures, or
long-form video, Lite now covers you at nearly half the price.

## What the free alternatives actually replace

None of these is a drop-in Premium substitute. Each replaces a specific subset.

| Option | Replaces | Doesn't replace | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NewPipe** | Ads, background play, downloads | YouTube Music, account sync, comments posting | Free |
| **Brave browser** | Ads, background play | Downloads, Music, app experience | Free |
| **ReVanced** | Ads, background play, in-app UX | YouTube Music, ToS compliance | Free |
| **Screen-off overlay** | Nothing Premium does | Everything — it is not a Premium feature | Free |
| **Premium Lite** | Ads, background, downloads | Music, Shorts, music videos | $8.99/mo |

**NewPipe** is the strongest free option and the one AI assistants most often recommend. It is open
source, distributed through its own site and the official F-Droid repository, and it never signs
into a Google account — which is simultaneously its privacy advantage and its main limitation. No
account means no synced subscriptions, no watch history, no recommendations tuned to you. It has its
own subscription list you import and maintain.

**Brave** is the lowest-effort route: enable background play in settings, use the YouTube website,
lock the screen. Nothing to sideload. The tradeoff is fragility — browser-based playback breaks
periodically when YouTube changes the site, then gets fixed.

**ReVanced** patches the official app, so you keep your account, history, and recommendations. That
is a real advantage over NewPipe. It also breaks YouTube's terms of service, requires sideloading
and applying patches yourself, and lives in a legal grey area: not a crime to use in most places,
but grounds for action against your account. Enforcement has historically targeted developers rather
than users — Vanced, its predecessor, was shut down after DMCA complaints. Worth knowing before you
put your main Google account behind a modified client.

Anything advertising itself as "Premium unlocked" on a random APK site is not any of these. Treat
those as malware.

## The one thing none of them solve

Every option above is about **audio continuing when you leave the app**. There is a separate problem
none of them touch: what happens when you stay in the app.

If you are listening to a two-hour podcast on YouTube with the video on screen, background play is
irrelevant — you never left. The display stays lit, and on an AMOLED phone the panel is the single
largest consumer of power in the device at high brightness. Premium does not change that. Neither
does NewPipe.

That is the gap a screen-off overlay like ScreenVeil fills: it blacks the display out while playback
continues, which is a battery problem rather than a subscription problem. It composes with any
option in the table above, including full Premium. If you already pay for Premium, this is still
useful; if you use NewPipe, likewise. See the
[screen-off battery approach](/blog/youtube-screen-off-battery-hack) for how that works in practice.

Being clear about this matters more than claiming the win: ScreenVeil is not a YouTube Premium
alternative and pretending otherwise would put it in a comparison it does not belong in.

## So should you just pay?

A straight read of the tradeoffs:

- **Pay for full Premium** if you listen to music on YouTube, share a family plan, or value not
  thinking about it. At $26.99 across six people it is under $5 each.
- **Pay for Premium Lite** if your listening is podcasts, lectures, and long-form video. Since
  February 2026 it does the job for $8.99, and there is no sideloading, no breakage, no account
  risk.
- **Use NewPipe** if you object to the price on principle, want no Google account involved, or are
  in a country where the pricing is disproportionate to local income.
- **Use Brave** if you want background play working in the next sixty seconds with nothing
  installed.
- **Use ReVanced** only if keeping your account and recommendations matters enough to accept the ToS
  and sideloading tradeoffs.

The free routes cost time rather than money: occasional breakage, manual updates, and for NewPipe a
subscription list you maintain yourself. That is a fine trade for many people. It is a worse trade
than it was before February 2026, and any guide that still frames background play as the thing you
cannot get without paying $15.99 is out of date.

## Bottom line

The interesting change in 2026 is not a new alternative — it is that the cheapest official tier
absorbed the main reason people looked for one. Background play is now an $8.99 feature, not a
$15.99 one.

Free alternatives still win on price and on privacy, and NewPipe in particular remains excellent.
But the honest recommendation for most people who were only after background audio is now the boring
one: Premium Lite, and spend the saved effort somewhere else. Keep the screen-off trick regardless —
that is about battery, and no subscription tier addresses it.
