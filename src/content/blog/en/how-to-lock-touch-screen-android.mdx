**Android does not have one "lock touch screen" switch.** It has several partial ones, and which you need
depends on the situation: a toddler watching cartoons needs something different from a podcast playing in
your pocket.

**Quick answer:**

- **Child watching video:** App pinning to keep them in the app, plus the player's own lock (Netflix) or
  Samsung's **Interaction control** to ignore taps.
- **Listening with the phone in a pocket or on the bed:** a **black-screen overlay that blocks touches**,
  such as ScreenVeil.
- **Phone in a pocket with the screen off:** Android's built-in **accidental touch protection**.

## Which method fits your situation?

| Situation | Best option | Picture stays visible? | Blocks taps in the app? | Built in? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Child watching Netflix | Netflix player Lock | Yes | Yes | Yes, in Netflix |
| Child watching any video app (Samsung) | Interaction control | Yes | Yes | Yes, Samsung |
| Child should not leave the app | App pinning | Yes | No | Yes, all Android |
| Listening to audio, phone in pocket or bed | Black-screen overlay (ScreenVeil) | No, screen is black | Yes | No, free app |
| Screen off in pocket | Accidental touch protection | Screen off | Yes | Yes, most phones |

## Option 1: The video app's own lock

**Netflix** has the cleanest version: while a video plays on your phone, tap the screen and choose **Lock**.
Taps are ignored until you tap the lock icon and confirm, per
[Netflix's help page](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/115018). It does not block system gestures, so a
child can still leave the app — pair it with App pinning. Some other streaming apps have similar buttons.

**YouTube does not, as a standard feature.** YouTube
[tested a Lock Screen option](https://www.phonearena.com/news/YouTubes-experimental-Lock-Screen-feature-could-eliminate-accidental-pauses_id148723)
for some Premium subscribers in 2023, which flashed a padlock instead of responding to taps. It was a
time-limited experiment, not a setting everyone has. If you see it in your player settings, use it; if not,
use one of the options below.

## Option 2: Samsung's Interaction control

Many Samsung Galaxy phones have a built-in touch block in accessibility settings:

1. Open **Settings → Accessibility → Interaction and dexterity → Interaction control**.
2. Turn it on and set the shortcut you will use to start and stop it.
3. Start the video and use the shortcut. You can block the whole screen or only some areas — for example the
   progress bar, so a child cannot skip, while leaving play and pause working.
4. While it is on, the phone also **stays in the current app**: Home and app switching are blocked. Use the
   shortcut again to unlock.

That makes Interaction control the closest thing Android has to a full toddler lock. Menu names vary slightly
by One UI version, and Samsung's
[support page](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10001944/) has the current steps. If your phone is not a Samsung, look in Accessibility for
something similar; it is not available on every Android phone.

## Option 3: App pinning (keeps a child in one app)

**App pinning stops a child leaving the app**, which is the other half of the toddler problem. It does not
block taps inside the app, so combine it with Option 1 or 2.

1. Turn it on: **Settings → Security** (or Security and privacy → More security settings) → **App pinning**,
   called **Pin app** on Samsung. Turn on "Ask for PIN before unpinning."
2. Open the app, then open **Recent apps**.
3. Tap the app's icon at the top of its card and choose **Pin**.
4. To unpin, hold Back and Recent apps together (or swipe up and hold with gesture navigation), then enter
   your PIN.

For younger children, pinning **YouTube Kids** rather than the main YouTube app also limits what they can tap
into.

## Option 4: A black-screen overlay that blocks touches

When you are **listening rather than watching** — a podcast while you walk, a lecture with the phone in your
pocket, an audiobook in bed — you do not need the picture at all. What you need is for nothing to pause,
skip or close it.

A black-screen overlay does that. [ScreenVeil](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.screenveil.app)
draws a black layer over the whole screen while the app underneath keeps playing, and **blocks every touch**
until you unlock it deliberately:

- **Touch blocking** while the overlay is up; touch the screen to reveal the unlock button.
- **Biometric unlock**, so only your fingerprint lifts the overlay — useful when a child grabs the phone.
- **Pocket mode** that blacks the screen out automatically when the phone goes in a pocket.
- **Sleep timer** that turns the screen black on its own as you drift off.
- A **clock** on the black screen so you can still check the time.

It needs one permission, "display over other apps," and on AMOLED and OLED phones the black pixels are
switched off, so it also uses less display power than leaving the picture on.

**When not to use it:** if a child is watching a video, a black screen defeats the purpose. Use Options 1 to 3.

## Option 5: Accidental touch protection (screen off)

Most Android phones have a setting that ignores touches when the phone is in a pocket or bag, detected by the
proximity sensor. Look in **Settings → Display** for **Accidental touch protection** (Samsung) or a similar
**Pocket mode**. It works when the screen is off, so it suits music apps that keep playing with the screen off,
but not apps that need the screen on.

## Common questions about locking the screen

**Will a touch lock stop a child pressing the volume or power buttons?** Usually not. Most touch locks only
affect the touchscreen.

**Does any of this need root?** No. Every option here works on a standard phone.

**Can a child get out of App pinning?** Not without your PIN, if you turned on "Ask for PIN before
unpinning." Without that option, the unpin gesture works for anyone who knows it.
