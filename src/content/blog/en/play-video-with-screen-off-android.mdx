**Lock your phone and most videos stop playing.** Android sends the app to the background, and many
video, browser and course apps pause there. Check whether your app has its own background or
audio-only option first. If it doesn't, keep the app open and turn the screen black instead: the video
keeps playing, you see nothing, and touches are ignored.

## Why videos stop when the screen turns off

When you press the power button, Android moves whatever is on screen into the background. Some apps
keep playing there, because their developers built background playback in. Many don't, because they
were made for watching, not listening. Users of Plex's Android app, for example, have asked on its
forum for screen-off video playback that the app doesn't offer
([Plex forum](https://forums.plex.tv/t/screen-off-video-play-on-android-app/905303)).

That's fine at a desk. It's a problem on a walk, on the bus, while cooking or at the gym — exactly when
you'd want to hear a lecture, a talk or a long video rather than watch it.

## Step 1: Check the app's own options

| App | Built-in option |
|---|---|
| YouTube | Background play with YouTube Premium |
| VLC | Plays sound in the background; open or share a video and play it as audio ([Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/news/use-trick-play-youtube-audio-phone-screen-off-3966982)) |
| Twitch | Background audio setting and Audio Only mode in the app |
| Course and lecture apps | Varies; look for background play, audio-only or downloadable audio |
| Mobile browsers | Usually pause when the screen locks |

If your app has one of these, use it. For YouTube specifically, see
[YouTube background play in 2026](/blog/youtube-background-play-2026-workaround).

## Step 2: Keep any app playing with a black screen

When the app has no background option, don't lock the phone. A black screen overlay draws a pure black
layer over your screen while the app underneath stays open. To Android, the video app is still on
screen, so it keeps playing. With [ScreenVeil](/apps/screenveil):

1. Install ScreenVeil and tap **Start Service**.
2. Allow **"display over other apps"** when Android asks. It is the only permission you grant.
3. Open your video and start playing it.
4. Tap ScreenVeil's floating button, or its Quick Settings tile. The screen turns black and the video
   keeps playing.
5. To get back, touch the screen, then tap unlock.

While the screen is black, touches are blocked, so your pocket can't pause or skip anything. Pocket
mode can also turn the screen black on its own when the phone goes into your pocket.

## Battery and data: what to expect

| Method | Screen power | Mobile data |
|---|---|---|
| Watching normally | Highest | Full video |
| App's audio-only mode | Screen can be off | Much lower |
| Black screen overlay (AMOLED/OLED) | Much lower | Full video |
| Black screen overlay (LCD) | Small saving | Full video |

On **AMOLED and OLED** screens, a pure black pixel is switched off, so a black overlay uses far less
screen power than a lit video. On **LCD** screens the backlight stays on. For the details, see
[does dark mode save battery on AMOLED?](/blog/does-dark-mode-save-battery-amoled)

## Example: listening to recorded lectures

Lectures are the most common reason people search for this: course apps and university portals rarely
play in the background, but a second listen to last week's lecture fits perfectly into a commute.

- **Speed it up a little.** Many players allow 1.25× or 1.5×; speech stays clear.
- **Use audio for review, not first contact.** Lectures heavy on slides, equations or code are better
  watched once, then replayed by ear.
- **Note timestamps.** When something clicks, write down the time so you can find the slide later.
- **Use headphones in public.**

## The bottom line

If an app has background play or an audio-only mode, use it. If it doesn't, keep the app open and black
out the screen instead: the video keeps playing, nothing lights up, and stray touches can't stop it.
