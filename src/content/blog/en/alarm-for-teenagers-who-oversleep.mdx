**Teenagers who oversleep usually aren't lazy — their body clock runs later.** The alarm goes off in
deep sleep, gets switched off by reflex, and nobody remembers it. What helps is an alarm that can't
be dismissed half-asleep, a little more sleep, and a plan the teen agreed to rather than one imposed.

## Why teens sleep through alarms

During adolescence the body clock shifts later. Teens tend to feel sleepy later at night, but school
still starts early. The result is a short night and an alarm that lands in deep sleep.

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends **8 to 10 hours of sleep** for teenagers aged 13 to
18. On school nights many get far less. A short night plus an early alarm makes "I didn't hear it"
honestly true: the sound got switched off before the brain properly woke up.

## What doesn't work

- **A louder ringtone.** Loud is good, but a one-tap off button still wins at 6:30 a.m.
- **Five alarms in a row.** It teaches the brain that the first four don't count.
- **A parent at the door every morning.** It works, but it becomes a daily argument and it doesn't
  last into adulthood.

## What works: an alarm that needs real attention

A **mission alarm** keeps ringing until a task is done: a math problem, a code to type, a number of
steps, or a barcode in another room. Because the task needs attention a half-asleep brain doesn't
have, it can't be done on autopilot. By the time the alarm stops, the teen is awake.

## The rest of the plan

- **Agree on it together.** Let the teen choose the Ritual and the song. An alarm they picked is one
  they'll fight less.
- **Protect the night before.** Phones out of bed and a regular bedtime help more than any alarm. If
  late-night scrolling is the issue, limit the feeds rather than banning the phone.
- **Keep weekends close.** Sleeping in three hours on Saturday makes Monday harder.
- **Check the alarm actually fires.** Some phones block alarms from apps; see
  [why Android alarms don't go off](/blog/why-android-alarms-dont-go-off-exact-alarms).

More ideas for deep sleepers: [how to stop sleeping through your alarm](/blog/how-to-stop-sleeping-through-your-alarm-android).

## The bottom line

A teenager who sleeps through alarms needs an alarm that can't be switched off half-asleep, and a
little more sleep. Set up a Ritual they chose, keep the volume up, and let the task do the waking
instead of a parent at the door.
