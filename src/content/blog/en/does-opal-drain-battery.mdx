**The mechanism says no. A lot of users say yes. Both can be true, and your own battery screen is
the only thing that settles it for your phone.**

Opal ran a local VPN on iOS until 2022, then moved to Apple's Screen Time API. On Android, its
[Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal) says it blocks through
the **AccessibilityService API**. Neither of those *should* be a battery-hungry mechanism.

But search this question and Google's AI Overview now answers **"Yes, Opal can cause notable battery
drain"**, built on Reddit threads where people report exactly that. Dismissing those reports because
the architecture looks cheap would be the wrong move, so this page takes them seriously first.

## What users actually report

The most-cited thread is a two-week test posted to r/nosurf, where the verdict was blunt:

> "It drains battery noticeably. Whatever it's running in the background to monitor your apps is
> hungry."

That is first-hand experience and it deserves more than a shrug. Two things can explain it without
either side being wrong:

- **Implementation, not mechanism.** The accessibility API delivers events cheaply, but what an app
  *does* on each event is up to the app. A blocker that wakes up, queries usage stats and re-renders
  state on every window change costs far more than one that filters early and sleeps.
- **The battery optimiser fighting back.** Aggressive power management on Samsung, Xiaomi and OnePlus
  devices repeatedly kills and restarts background apps. The restart cycle itself burns power, and it
  gets attributed to the app being restarted.

So the honest version is narrower than either "no" or "yes": **the mechanism does not require a
battery cost, and some users clearly experience one anyway.**

## Where the question came from

Until 2022, Opal on iOS worked by running a **local VPN**. That is a genuine battery cost: the tunnel
stays alive, traffic is inspected, and the radio gets less idle time. People noticed, wrote about it,
and some of the reputation outlived the architecture.

It is also why "does *this* blocker drain battery" is worth asking about any blocker — the baseline
cost depends on the method, even if the real-world cost depends on the build.

## What each blocking method costs

| Method | Battery cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **VPN-based** | Medium to high | A tunnel runs continuously and inspects traffic |
| **Accessibility-based** | Low | Reacts to events the system already emits |
| **DNS filtering** | Negligible | One lookup per domain, no persistent process |
| **Built-in timers** | Negligible | The OS was already counting |

Opal on Android is in the second row. So is [Mindful Guard](/apps/mindful-guard), AppBlock, and most
of the category. [Freedom](/blog/freedom-alternative-android) uses the first on mobile, which is the
trade-off it makes for covering every app at the network layer.

## Check your own phone in thirty seconds

Do not take anyone's word for this, including ours. Your phone already knows.

1. **Android:** Settings → Battery → **Battery usage**. **iPhone:** Settings → **Battery**.
2. Set the range to the **last 24 hours or 7 days**. A single hour tells you nothing.
3. Find the blocker in the list.
4. Compare it against **Display**.

On nearly every phone, the screen is the largest single consumer by a wide margin. If a blocker is a
small fraction of it, the blocker is not what is draining your battery. If it is sitting near the
top, that is worth investigating — and that applies to any app, not just a blocker.

## What is actually draining your battery

In rough order, on most phones:

- **The screen**, especially at high brightness. We went through the measured numbers in
  [does dark mode save battery on OLED](/blog/does-dark-mode-save-battery-amoled).
- **Cellular data on a weak signal**, where the radio ramps up to stay connected.
- **GPS and location**, when something is using it continuously.
- **Video**, which combines the screen, the radio and the decoder.

A focus app that reacts when you open Instagram is not in this category. There is an irony worth
naming: a blocker that genuinely reduces your screen time **saves** more power than it spends.

## If a blocker really is draining your phone

Three things usually explain it:

- **It is VPN-based and the tunnel never sleeps.** Switching to an accessibility-based blocker
  removes the cost entirely.
- **It is badly built and polls constantly** instead of waiting for events. The battery list will
  show this clearly.
- **Your phone's battery optimiser is fighting it** — repeatedly killing and restarting the app,
  which costs more than leaving it alone. Exempting the blocker from battery optimisation often
  *lowers* its usage.

## The bottom line

The architecture Opal uses today does not require a battery cost, and as a category,
accessibility-based blockers are cheap. That is not the same as promising your phone will be fine —
users report otherwise, often enough that search engines now lead with it, and implementation
differences are a real explanation rather than an excuse.

Which is why the answer is not a claim at all. It is a thirty-second check on your own device, where
the screen is almost certainly the thing at the top of the list.
