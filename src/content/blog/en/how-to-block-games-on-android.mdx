**You have three good ways to block games on Android.** Digital Wellbeing's app timer caps each game
per day. Google Family Link lets parents control a child's phone. And an app blocker can block every
game on a weekly schedule or for a focus session, with no override button while the block runs.

## Why games are harder to put down than other apps

Mobile games are designed to bring you back: daily rewards, energy that refills, streaks, events that
end soon. "One quick round" during a study break easily becomes forty minutes. Uninstalling works until
the next update, sale or event pulls you back, and then you lose your progress for nothing.

Blocking is the middle ground: the game stays installed, your progress is safe, but it won't open when
you've decided it shouldn't.

## Option 1: Digital Wellbeing's app timer (built in)

On most Android phones: **Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls → Dashboard**, pick a game
and set an **app timer**. When the time is used up, the game is paused until midnight.

- **Good for:** a light daily cap on one or two games.
- **Falls short:** one game at a time, no weekly schedule, and the timer is easy to switch off again in
  Settings when you want "one more round".

## Option 2: Google Family Link (for a child's phone)

**Family Link** lets a parent manage a child's Android phone from their own phone. To block a game, open
Family Link, pick your child, then **Controls → App limits**, choose the game and tap **Block**. Google
says the block applies within about five minutes, and a child who is playing gets a one-minute warning
first ([Google Family Link Help](https://support.google.com/families/answer/7103028)). It works on Android 7 and later.

To stop new games being downloaded, turn on **parental controls in the Play Store**: profile picture →
**Settings → Family → Parental controls**, create a PIN and set an age rating for Apps & games
([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/1075738)).

- **Good for:** parents of younger children.
- **Falls short:** it is built to manage someone else's phone, not your own, and it needs a supervised
  Google account. Play Store parental controls filter downloads; they don't block games already
  installed.

## Which option to pick

| You want to… | Best option |
|---|---|
| Cap one game a little each day | Digital Wellbeing app timer |
| Control a child's phone | Google Family Link |
| Block all games during work, study or bedtime | An app blocker with a weekly schedule |
| Block games for one focused session | An app blocker's focus timer |

## Make the block stick

- **Block the hours, not the game forever.** Keep a window for playing, such as weekend afternoons. A
  ban invites workarounds; a plan doesn't.
- **Turn off game notifications.** Events and "your energy is full" alerts are the cue that starts the
  habit.
- **Block the bedtime hour too.** Late-night sessions cost sleep as well as time.
- **Watch for the swap.** If you stop gaming and start scrolling, limit the feeds as well:
  [how to limit Reels per day](/blog/limit-reels-per-day-android).

## The bottom line

Digital Wellbeing is fine for a light cap, and Family Link is the right tool for a child's phone. To
keep your own games closed during the hours that matter, block them on a schedule or for a focus
session with an app blocker that has no override button.
