**Android sets screen time limits per app, not for the whole phone.** Use Digital Wellbeing's app timers
for daily caps on the apps that eat your time, Bedtime mode to keep nights free, and Google Family Link if
it's a child's phone. When you need a limit you can't switch off in a weak moment, use an app blocker with
no override button.

## Why limit screen time per app

Total screen time includes maps, messages, calls and work. What most people want to cut is the handful of
apps that turn five minutes into fifty: social feeds, short videos and games. Estimates of average daily
smartphone use sit at roughly four to five hours a day, depending on the study
([Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/blog/smartphone-usage-stats)) — and most of the
regrettable part sits in a few apps. That's why app-by-app limits work better than one big number.

## Option 1: Digital Wellbeing app timers (built in)

On most Android phones:

1. Open **Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls**.
2. Tap the **Dashboard** (or the app list under the usage chart).
3. Pick an app and tap **App timer** (the hourglass).
4. Choose a daily limit, for example 30 minutes.

When the time is used up, the app's icon turns grey and it's paused until midnight
([Google Android guide](https://guidebooks.google.com/android/exploremorefeatures/settimersforyourapps)).

**On Samsung:** Settings → **Digital Wellbeing and parental controls**, tap an app under the chart, then
**App timer**.

**Limits:** one app at a time, no weekly schedule, and the timer can be raised or removed in Settings
whenever you want "just a bit more".

## Option 2: Bedtime mode and Focus mode

- **Bedtime mode** (Digital Wellbeing → Bedtime mode) turns the screen grey and silences notifications on
  a schedule you set. It limits *when* you use the phone, not *how long*.
- **Focus mode** (Digital Wellbeing → Focus mode) pauses the apps you choose while it's on. It's useful for
  a work block, but you can tap **Take a break** and the apps come back.

## Option 3: Google Family Link (for a child's phone)

**Family Link** lets a parent manage a child's Android phone from their own: a **daily limit** for the
whole device, limits on specific apps, a bedtime and approval of new downloads. The child can't change
these limits on their own phone. It needs a supervised Google account and is built for parents, not for
limiting your own phone.

## Which option to use

| You want to… | Best option |
|---|---|
| Cap one or two apps a little each day | Digital Wellbeing app timer |
| Keep the phone quiet at night | Bedtime mode |
| Block distractions for one work session | Focus mode, or an app blocker's focus timer |
| Limit a child's whole phone | Google Family Link |
| Limit only the Reels or Shorts feed | An app blocker with feed-level limits |
| A limit you can't undo in a weak moment | An app blocker with no override button |

## Starter limits that stick

- **Pick your top three apps**, not everything. Look at the Dashboard and cap the three you spend the
  most time in.
- **Use a per-session limit for feeds.** A daily cap alone can be spent in one long scroll; see
  [the session method for limiting Reels](/blog/limit-reels-per-day-android).
- **Keep the last hour before bed free** of social apps with Bedtime mode or a schedule.
- **Review after a week** and tighten what felt easy.

For a deeper look at the built-in tools, see
[Digital Wellbeing vs third-party focus apps](/blog/android-digital-wellbeing-vs-third-party-focus-apps).

## The bottom line

Android's Digital Wellbeing covers the basics: app timers per app, Bedtime mode for nights and Family Link
for children. Its weakness is that a limit on your own phone is easy to undo. When that's the problem, an
app blocker with no override button turns the limit into one that holds.
