**Opal is available on Android** — it has been on Google Play since mid-2025, with 500K+ downloads. So
the question is not whether you can get it, but whether it is worth the subscription. Its free tier is
limited, and Opal Pro costs about $100 a year.

**Quick answer:** the best Opal alternatives for Android in 2026 are **Mindful Guard** (best free option,
with strict mode and Reels/Shorts limits), **AppBlock** (best for location and Wi-Fi rules) and
**Freedom** (best if you also need to block your computer). If you want Opal's polish and streaks and
are happy to pay, Opal itself is a reasonable choice.

## Opal on Android: the facts first

A lot of comparison pages, including an earlier version of this one, describe Opal as a VPN-based app.
That is out of date. Here is what Opal on Android actually is, from its
[Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal), checked in
October 2026:

| | Opal on Android |
|---|---|
| Available since | Mid-2025 |
| Downloads | 500K+ |
| Minimum Android version | Android 10 |
| How it blocks apps | AccessibilityService API (on Android 14+) |
| Data | "Your data never leaves your device," per the listing |
| Price | Free download, limited free tier; Pro by subscription |
| In-app purchases on Google Play | $2.99 to $79.99 per item |
| Pro price (iOS, per third-party reviews) | About $99.99/year, or $399 lifetime |

**Does Opal drain battery?** Not in any way that sets it apart. Opal used a local VPN on iOS until
2022, when it moved to Apple's Screen Time API. On Android it uses the accessibility service, the same
mechanism Mindful Guard and AppBlock use. If a blocker is draining your phone, Settings → Battery will
show it; for accessibility-based blockers that is rarely the case.

**Is Opal free?** Partly. The free version blocks apps with a limited number of rules. The setting that
stops you ending a session early, which is the reason most people install a blocker, is on the paid
side according to
[third-party reviews](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/opal-app-review).

## Mindful Guard (best free Opal alternative)

Mindful Guard is built for Android only. What makes it a fit for people leaving Opal is that the parts
Opal charges for are in the free plan: scheduled blocks and a **Strict Mode with no early exit** — once a
session starts, you cannot cancel it from inside the app.

It also does something neither Opal nor AppBlock lists: **Reel Guard** limits the short-form feed *inside*
Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, Facebook and Snapchat, so you can keep messages and search but cap Reels and
Shorts per day or per session. If your problem is the feed rather than the app, that matters more than
anything else in this table.

- **Who it's for:** Android users who want strict blocking without a subscription, or who mainly need
  to stop Reels and Shorts.
- **Who should skip it:** iPhone users, and anyone who needs to block a laptop too — use Freedom.
- **Privacy:** no account, no ads, no data collected; it works offline.
- **Pricing:** free plan; Premium with monthly, yearly or lifetime options.

## AppBlock (best for rules by place or Wi-Fi)

AppBlock is one of the most established Android blockers. Its strength is rules: block TikTok when you
arrive at the office, or block games only on your home Wi-Fi. Its Strict Mode is hard to get around.

The free version limits how many schedules you can create and prompts you to upgrade often. On phones
with aggressive battery savers (Xiaomi, some Samsung models), you may need to exempt it from battery
optimization so it is not stopped in the background — true of most blockers.

- **Who it's for:** people who want blocks triggered by location or network.
- **Who should skip it:** anyone who wants a generous free tier.

## Freedom (best for phone and computer together)

Freedom's selling point is cross-device blocking: one schedule locks your phone, Mac and Windows PC at
once. On Android it blocks through a local VPN, which is how it can filter websites as well as apps.
Locked Mode stops you ending a session early.

- **Who it's for:** writers, developers and remote workers who switch devices to get around a block.
- **Who should skip it:** mobile-only users, or anyone who wants a free option. See our full
  [Freedom alternatives for Android](/blog/freedom-alternative-android) comparison.

## One Sec (best for gentle friction)

One Sec does not block anything outright. It makes you pause and breathe for a few seconds before an
app opens, which is surprisingly effective at breaking muscle-memory opening. For deeper habits, many
people simply wait out the pause.

- **Who it's for:** mild habits you want to notice rather than forbid.
- **Who should skip it:** anyone who needs a hard lock.

## Digital Wellbeing (free, built in)

Every modern Android phone already has Digital Wellbeing: daily app timers, Focus mode and Bedtime
mode. It is free and needs no install. The catch is enforcement — when a timer runs out, you can extend
or disable it in a couple of taps, which is exactly when willpower is lowest. We compared it in detail in
[Digital Wellbeing vs third-party focus apps](/blog/android-digital-wellbeing-vs-third-party-focus-apps).

## Stay Focused (best for daily time budgets)

Stay Focused (not to be confused with the StayFocusd Chrome extension) gives you a daily allowance per
app or group of apps — say 45 minutes for all social media — and blocks them once it is spent.

- **Who it's for:** people who do better with an allowance than with blocked hours.
- **Who should skip it:** anyone who wants location rules or feed-level limits.
