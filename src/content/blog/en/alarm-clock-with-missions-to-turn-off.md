**A mission alarm clock is an alarm that won't switch off until you finish a task — math, a code, steps, a barcode scan, or push-ups.** Because the task needs attention your half-asleep brain doesn't have, you can't dismiss it by reflex. That single design change is why a mission alarm wakes heavy sleepers when a louder ringtone and a snooze button don't.

## The problem with the snooze button

Snooze asks for one thing: a sleepy tap. Your brain in deep sleep is perfectly capable of providing that tap and forgetting it happened. Worse, each snooze drops you into fragmented, low-quality sleep, so you wake groggier than if you'd gotten up the first time. The snooze button is optimized for staying asleep.

## Why a task works

Sleep inertia — the grogginess right after waking — fades with **arousal**, either cognitive or physical. A mission forces that arousal:

- **Cognitive missions** (math, memory, typing, trivia, sequences) require focus and accuracy you can't fake while drowsy.
- **Physical missions** (steps, shake, walking to a barcode, push-ups) get your body upright and your heart rate up.

Either way, by the time the alarm goes quiet, you're actually awake. The alarm has become a wake-up *test* rather than a noise you can swat away.
