**Lock your phone and most lecture videos stop playing.** That's because Android sends the app to the
background, and many video and course apps pause there. If the app has no background play, the
simplest fix is to keep it open and turn the screen black instead: the lecture keeps playing, you
see nothing, and touches are ignored.

## Why lectures pause when the screen goes off

Recorded lectures reach you through all kinds of apps: a university portal in the browser, a course
platform's own app, a shared video file, or YouTube. When you press the power button, Android moves
whatever is on screen into the background. Some apps keep playing there. Many pause, because they
were built for watching, not listening.

That is fine at a desk. It is a problem on a walk, on the bus, while cooking or at the gym, which is
exactly when a second listen to last week's lecture would fit.

## Check the app first

Before installing anything, look for these in your course or video app:

- **Background play or audio-only mode.** Some apps have it in their player or settings.
- **Downloadable audio.** Some platforms offer an audio file or podcast version of lectures.
- **A transcript.** Not for listening, but useful when you only need one section.

If one of these exists, use it. If not, keep the video playing with the screen black.

## Keep the lecture playing with a black screen

A black screen overlay draws a pure black layer over your screen while the app underneath stays open.
To Android, the lecture app is still on screen, so it keeps playing. With
[ScreenVeil](/apps/screenveil):

1. Install ScreenVeil and tap **Start Service**.
2. Allow **"display over other apps"** when Android asks. It is the only permission you grant.
3. Open your lecture and start playing it.
4. Tap ScreenVeil's floating button, or its Quick Settings tile. The screen turns black and the
   lecture keeps playing.
5. To get back, touch the screen, then tap unlock.

While the screen is black, touches are blocked, so your pocket can't pause or skip the lecture.
Pocket mode can also turn the screen black on its own when the phone goes into your pocket.

## Battery: what to expect

On **AMOLED and OLED** screens, a pure black pixel is switched off, so a black overlay uses less
power than a lit video. The brighter your screen was and the longer the lecture, the bigger the
difference. On **LCD** screens the backlight stays on, so the saving is small. For the details, see
[does dark mode save battery on AMOLED?](/blog/does-dark-mode-save-battery-amoled)

## Tips for studying by ear

- **Speed it up a little.** Many players let you play at 1.25× or 1.5×; speech stays clear and you
  get through more.
- **Use audio for review, not first contact.** Lectures heavy on slides, equations or code are
  better watched once, then replayed by ear.
- **Keep a note open for later.** When something clicks, write a timestamp so you can find the slide
  afterwards.
- **Use headphones in public.** Your classmates on the bus will thank you.

## The bottom line

If your course app pauses when you lock the phone and has no audio-only option, keep it open and
black out the screen instead. The lecture keeps playing, nothing lights up, and stray touches can't
stop it.
