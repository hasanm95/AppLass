**Turn on Extra dim first.** It's built into Android and takes the screen below the normal minimum
brightness. If it's still too bright for a dark bedroom, a dim overlay lowers it further. And if you
only need sound, a fully black screen removes the light entirely.

## Why the lowest brightness isn't low enough

Phone makers set the minimum brightness so the screen stays readable in most rooms. In a fully dark
bedroom, on a night flight or in a cinema, that minimum still feels bright, and your eyes have to
adjust every time you look at the phone.

## Step 1: Turn on Extra dim

Android 12 added a setting for exactly this, under Accessibility, because some phones can't get dim enough for low-light use ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/android-15-even-dimmer-3436221)). Most Android phones from Android 12 on have it:

1. Open **Settings** and search for **"Extra dim"**. On many phones it is under **Accessibility**;
   on Samsung it is also called **Extra dim**.
2. Turn it on and adjust its **intensity**.
3. Add the **Extra dim** tile to Quick Settings so you can switch it on and off in one tap.

Menu locations vary between phone brands and Android versions, which is why searching Settings is the
quickest way to find it.

**Pair it with Night Light** (Eye comfort shield on Samsung) if you also want a warmer screen. Night
Light reduces blue light; it doesn't make the screen much darker on its own.

## Step 2: Add a dim overlay if it's still too bright

A dim overlay draws a semi-transparent dark layer over everything, so the screen looks darker
again on top of Extra dim. [ScreenVeil](/apps/screenveil) has a **dim mode** for this: a low glow
instead of full black, for when you still need to see the screen.

One thing to know: on an **LCD** screen, an overlay darkens the picture but the backlight keeps
running at the same level, so it saves little battery. On **AMOLED and OLED** screens, darker pixels
really do draw less power.

## Step 3: Go fully black when you only need sound

If you are listening rather than watching (a podcast, a lecture, music, a video you fall asleep to),
you don't need any light at all. ScreenVeil's black screen covers the display with pure black while
the app underneath keeps playing:

1. Install ScreenVeil, tap **Start Service** and allow **"display over other apps"**.
2. Start your audio or video.
3. Tap the floating button or the Quick Settings tile. The screen turns black.
4. Touch the screen and tap unlock to get back.

On AMOLED screens the black pixels are switched off, so the screen gives off no light and uses less
power. Touches are blocked while it is black, so a sleepy hand can't pause anything. The time and date
can stay visible on the black screen if you want to check the hour.

## Which option to pick

| Situation | Best option |
|---|---|
| Reading in bed | Extra dim + Night Light |
| Extra dim still too bright | Extra dim + a dim overlay |
| Listening, not watching | A fully black screen |
| Cinema, plane, shared room | A fully black screen, or Extra dim at full intensity |

## The bottom line

Android's Extra dim solves most "too bright at night" problems and costs nothing. A dim overlay takes
it further when you still need to see the screen. When you only need the sound, a black screen is the
darkest option there is.
