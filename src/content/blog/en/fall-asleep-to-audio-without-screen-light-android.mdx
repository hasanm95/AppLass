**The best way to fall asleep to audio is with the screen black and a timer that stops playback.** A bright
screen next to your pillow works against sleep, and audio that plays until 4 a.m. drains your battery and
can wake you up.

**Quick answer:** use the app's own **sleep timer** if it has one, turn the screen **fully black** while it
plays, and **block touches** so rolling over does not pause, skip or open something else.

## Why keep the screen dark?

**Light at bedtime tells your body it is still daytime.** In a
[study of 116 adults](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/96/3/E463/2833657) (Gooley et al., 2011),
ordinary room light in the hours before bed delayed the onset of melatonin, the hormone that helps you fall
asleep, in 99% of participants. In a
[separate lab study](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1418490112), people who read on a light-emitting
tablet before bed took longer to fall asleep than when they read a printed book.

A phone at low brightness is dimmer than a room light, so the effect is smaller. But the easiest fix is also
the most complete: **if you are listening, you do not need the picture at all.**

## Step 1: Set a sleep timer in the app

Most audio apps have a sleep timer that stops playback after a set time or at the end of an episode:

| App | Sleep timer? | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| **Spotify** | Yes | Now playing → three dots → Sleep timer |
| **YouTube Music** | Yes | Now playing → three dots → Sleep timer |
| **Pocket Casts, most podcast apps** | Yes | Player screen, usually a moon or timer icon |
| **Audible, most audiobook apps** | Yes | Player screen → Sleep timer |
| **YouTube** | Some accounts | Player settings, if your account has it; it started as a 2024 Premium test |
| **Most video apps** | Usually no | Use a system or overlay timer instead |

Choose 30 to 45 minutes, or "end of episode" for podcasts. If you regularly wake up when the audio stops,
try a longer timer rather than none.

## Step 2: Turn the screen fully black

For apps that keep playing with the screen off — Spotify, podcast and audiobook apps — simply lock the phone.

For apps that **stop when the screen turns off** — YouTube on a free account, many video apps, some browsers —
the app has to stay in the foreground. The way to do that without light is a **black-screen overlay**: the
app keeps playing underneath while the screen shows pure black.

[ScreenVeil](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.screenveil.app) is a free overlay built for
this. Its features fit bedtime specifically:

- **Sleep timer** that blacks out the screen automatically after a delay, so you can fall asleep watching and
  let it go dark on its own.
- **Dim mode** for nights when full black is too much and you want a faint glow.
- **Clock on the black screen**, so a glance shows the time without full brightness.
- **Touch blocking**, so rolling onto the phone does not pause or skip.

On an **AMOLED or OLED** screen, black pixels are switched off, so the screen gives off essentially no light
and uses very little power. On an **LCD** phone the backlight stays on behind the black, so some light leaks
through; turn brightness to minimum as well.

## Step 3: Make sure it stops

An overlay keeps the screen dark, but it does not stop the audio. Pair it with something that does:

- the **app's own sleep timer** from Step 1, or
- the video simply ending — set a single long video rather than autoplay, or turn **autoplay off**, or
- a **sleep-timer app** from the Play Store, which pauses whatever is playing after a set time — the fallback
  for apps without their own timer.

## Step 4: Set up Bedtime mode around it

Android's **Bedtime mode** (Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls → Bedtime mode) silences
notifications and can turn the screen grayscale on a schedule, so a message at 1 a.m. does not light up the
phone. If you tend to start scrolling instead of listening, pair it with a scheduled app block — see
[how to stop doomscrolling at night](/blog/how-to-stop-doomscrolling-at-night).

| What | Why | How long to set up |
